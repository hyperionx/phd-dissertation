\begin{impact}      

\noindent This thesis lays the groundwork for practical neuromorphic computing by showing how memristive silicon oxide devices can enable biorealistic learning in artificial intelligence systems. Its findings will help researchers design hardware that learns and adapts more like the brain, using physical principles rather than abstract algorithms. \\

\noindent By revealing how silicon oxide memristors operate in the subthreshold regime, this work points the way to energy-efficient synaptic devices. These devices can support both potentiation and depression, key features for building hardware that mimics biological learning. The compact models developed here will allow engineers to simulate and optimize circuits before fabrication, speeding the path from concept to prototype. \\

\noindent At the system level, the thesis demonstrates that spiking neural networks can learn robustly when built on hardware-aware, biorealistic rules. This approach will guide future efforts to integrate physical constraints into artificial intelligence, making systems more reliable and efficient. \\

\noindent The research offers a clear blueprint for building neuromorphic systems that unite material science, circuit design, and learning algorithms. Its impact will extend to the development of low-power, adaptive computing devices for edge applications, robotics, and beyond. By connecting device physics to functional cognition, this thesis will shape the next generation of brain-inspired hardware.\\
