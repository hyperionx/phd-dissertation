\begin{impact}      

\noindent This thesis asks whether a material's intrinsic physics can enable synaptic learning from spike history rather than have it imposed through an abstract algorithm. The slow current transient of silicon oxide devices is taken as that learning state rather than as a defect, so the impact falls in how such materials are characterised and reported, and in how learning systems built from them are designed.\\

\noindent At the device level, the work appears in three peer-reviewed venues and three conference presentations listed below, with a best presentation award at IEEE SEAI 2025. Subthreshold characterisation points towards low-voltage
synapses that both potentiate and depress under a single polarity, and the compact model developed here lets others simulate candidate circuits before committing to fabrication.\\

\noindent The methodological contribution reaches further. Where a material supplies the learning variable, that variable cannot be reported alone, because the observation window and baseline convention used to obtain it change what the material is taken to compute. Refitting the same measurements under different conventions moves the fitted shape across a qualitative boundary. That explains disagreements between published fits of one phenomenon, and it gives other groups a route to falsification, since the conditions travel with the parameter.\\

\noindent At the system level, measured device dynamics constrain not only the local learning state but the behaviour a task produces, and that state can be held in the material rather than stored externally. Credit
is carried through a simulated two-layer network without weight transport. Neither the comparison nor the simulations claim that biology learns this way, and edge and robotics gains remain implementation targets. What the thesis establishes is a measured, falsifiable correspondence between material and learning, since intrinsic dynamics decide what spike history a synapse retains, and so what a delayed reward can reinforce.\\
