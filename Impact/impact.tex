\begin{impact}      

\noindent This thesis shows how memristive silicon-oxide devices can support biorealistic learning through physical dynamics rather than only stored numerical state. Subthreshold characterisation points towards low-voltage synapses supporting potentiation and depression, while the compact models let engineers simulate candidate circuits before fabrication. No circuit-level energy or area comparison is claimed. \\

\noindent At system level, simulated spiking networks learn on these hardware-aware rules, with architecture determining how the slow transient is used: homeostatic suppression aids fully connected networks, while device-constrained retention serves as neuronal leak. The transient also acts as a device-local analogue of an eligibility state. Fair controls identify its distinctive contribution as an interval-selective temporal prior that can avoid an externally stored eligibility trajectory under an in-material implementation. \\

\noindent Two features speak to on-chip learning. Local feedback alignment carries credit through a synthetic deep network without weight transport, stabilised by the same homeostatic mechanism used for static inference. Separately, public biological data provide a coherent dopamine-learning spine: Coddington supplies positive causal policy-learning evidence, Jeong recovers the inhibition direction, and leakage-safe DANDI 001340 replay improves held-out prediction over shuffled waveforms. \\

\noindent Reframing device physics as a computational resource folds physical constraints into learning-system design. Model comparison resolves a compressed rise without identifying cascade depth, and frozen pulse-off predictions state how the material--algorithm link can be falsified. The immediate value is a testable framework for matching material timescales to temporal-credit problems; low-power edge or robotics benefits remain implementation targets rather than measured outcomes. \\
