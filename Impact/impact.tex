\begin{impact}      

\noindent This thesis shows how memristive silicon-oxide devices can support biorealistic learning: hardware that learns through physical principles rather than abstract algorithms, at greater energy efficiency than conventional architectures. Characterising these devices in the subthreshold regime points towards energy-efficient synapses supporting both potentiation and depression, and the compact models developed here let engineers simulate such circuits before fabrication. \\

\noindent At the system level, spiking neural networks learn robustly on these hardware-aware rules, and how the device's slow transient should be treated depends on the architecture: suppressed by a homeostatic regulariser it aids fully connected networks, while used as a neuron's membrane leak it supplies a fabrication-set temporal memory. The same transient then acts as an eligibility trace, turning a discarded effect into the credit-assignment mechanism for reward-modulated reinforcement learning, matching the established algorithmic traces while supplying its timescale as a fabrication-set physical parameter. \\

\noindent Two features speak directly to on-chip learning. Paired with a local feedback-alignment signal, the trace carries credit through a deep network entirely locally, with no weight transport -- a long-standing obstacle to analogue-hardware training -- made reliable by the same homeostatic mechanism the device needs for stable inference in the static case. And the rule grounds in biology: it reproduces the human positive- versus negative-feedback asymmetry, and the full stack learns under a reward decoded from a directly measured dopamine transient (public mouse fibre-photometry, not measured here). \\

\noindent Reframing device physics as a computational resource rather than a limitation to be suppressed offers a way to fold physical constraints into the design of learning systems, uniting materials science, circuit design, and learning algorithms, with reach into low-power adaptive computing for edge and robotics applications. \\
