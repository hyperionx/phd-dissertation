\chapter{Memristive Reinforcement Learning}\label{ch:learning}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} trained static weights robust to the device's \emph{steady-state} non-idealities, setting the slow current transient aside. This chapter takes the opposite view: the transient of Chapters~\ref{ch:transients} and~5 is not a non-ideality to suppress but the computational substrate itself. The dispersive trap-cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:5p3} is shown to implement an \emph{eligibility trace}, the slowly decaying synaptic memory reward-modulated learning needs to bridge the action--reward delay. It is evaluated through a pre-registered ladder of experiments, with criteria fixed in advance and negative results reported. A trace-level model and a leaky integrate-and-fire (\abbrev{LIF}) network establish that retention sets the learnable delay. A contextual bandit and a sequential T-maze then close a reinforcement-learning loop and extend it to multi-step credit, benchmarked against the established reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity (R-STDP) and e-prop traces.\\

\noindent A deep all-local two-layer agent finally tests depth, reaching reliability only once a local homeostatic stabiliser is added, so one device family supplies both of biology's slow local primitives. The rule is then grounded in dopaminergic learning in two ways: \emph{behaviourally}, it reproduces the human positive- versus negative-feedback asymmetry on the Frank task, and \emph{mechanistically}, the capstone learns under a reward decoded from a directly measured dopamine transient, recorded in mice and reused from a public dataset rather than measured here. A testable retention--delay scaling relation runs throughout, as does the timescale regime in which the device-to-trace mapping is faithful.\\

\noindent The contributions of this chapter are entirely the author's own. The identification of the device transient as an eligibility trace, the three-factor learning rule built on it, and every experiment, simulation, and analysis reported here were conceived and carried out by the author, building on the device model of Chapter~\ref{ch:model} and the homeostatic mechanism of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}.

\section[The Transient as an Eligibility Trace]{The Transient as an Eligibility Trace}
\label{sec:7p1}

\noindent Reinforcement learning in any spiking substrate faces the \emph{distal-reward problem} of Section~\ref{subsec:learning-paradigms} \cite{izhikevich2007solving}: a reward arrives well after the spike coincidences that earned it. The established biological resolution is the three-factor, or neo-Hebbian, eligibility-trace rule \cite{fremaux2016neuromodulated, gerstner2018eligibility}. In that rule a fast pre--post coincidence sets a slowly decaying \emph{eligibility trace} that a delayed neuromodulator later gates into a weight change. Where the slow trace comes from is usually left open, the trace posited as an abstract synaptic variable. This chapter supplies it physically, showing the device transient is that trace. Its decay time constant, the device retention $\tau_{leak}$, sets the longest action--reward delay the network can learn \cite{izhikevich2007solving, florian2007reinforcement}.

\subsection[Repurposing the Trap-Cascade Model]{Repurposing the Trap-Cascade Model}
\label{sec:7p1p1}

\noindent The dispersive trap-cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:5p3} realises this trace directly. There, the gold-device transient was modelled as a cascade of $k\!\approx\!3$ sequential trap states, each charged by the one before it and leaking to ground through $R_{leak}$ (Equations~\ref{eq:5.6}--\ref{eq:5.7}), producing the compressed-exponential conductance rise and a retention time $\tau_{leak} = R_{leak}C$. In Chapter~5 that model is \emph{fitted} to a measured current transient under constant bias. Here the same dynamical system is \emph{driven} by a different input, a pre--post spike coincidence, and read out as a normalised gating variable. This turns a device-characterisation model into a synaptic learning primitive.\\

\noindent Concretely, the same system of first-order ordinary differential equations (\abbrev{ODE}s) is retained, but a coincidence signal $d_{ij}(t)$ at synapse $ij$ replaces the applied bias as the cascade drive, and the normalised final stage is read out as the eligibility trace,
\begin{align}
    \frac{dv^{(m)}_{ij}}{dt} &= \alpha\, p^{(m)}_{ij}\left( V_{max} - \left| v^{(m)}_{ij} \right| \right) - \frac{v^{(m)}_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}}, \label{eq:7.1} \\
    \frac{du_{ij}}{dt} &= \alpha\, d_{ij}\left( V_{max} - \left| u_{ij} \right| \right) - \frac{u_{ij}}{\tau_d}, \label{eq:7.2} \\
    e_{ij}(t) &= \frac{v^{(k)}_{ij}(t)}{V_{max}}, \label{eq:7.3}
\end{align}
with $p^{(1)}_{ij} = d_{ij}$ and $p^{(m)}_{ij} = v^{(m-1)}_{ij}/V_{max}$ for $m>1$ (the sequential cascade of Section~\ref{sec:5p3}, the upstream stage entering as a dimensionless occupancy fraction), and $\alpha = k/\tau_r$ the per-stage rate set by the fitted rise time. Equation~\ref{eq:7.2} is the slow space-charge state $u_{ij}$ that Chapter~5 separates from the trap chain, driven by the same coincidence $d_{ij}$ but relaxing at the proton-decay constant $\tau_d \gg \tau_{leak}$. It screens the interfacial field but carries no appreciable current. The eligibility is read from the final cascade stage alone (Equation~\ref{eq:7.3}), so $u_{ij}$ does not enter it. The $\left| \cdot \right|$ headroom term admits signed (depressing) excursions bounded to $\left| v \right|, \left| u \right| \le V_{max}$, a generalisation of Equations~\ref{eq:5.6}--\ref{eq:5.7} required because an eligibility trace must decrease as well as increase. The rise is the same sigmoidal trap-filling of Chapter~5 and the relaxation the same $R_{leak}$ leakage. Only the interpretation changes. The retention $\tau_{leak}$, a short-term-memory time in Chapter~5, becomes the \emph{credit-assignment window}: the longest coincidence-to-reward delay over which the synapse remains eligible.\\

\noindent The identification is mechanistic, not merely formal, and reduces exactly to the textbook trace in the appropriate limit. Take a single state ($k=1$, so $p^{(1)}_{ij} = d_{ij}$) and the small-signal regime $|v| \ll V_{max}$, where the headroom factor $V_{max} - |v| \to V_{max}$ is constant. Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} becomes linear,
\begin{align}
    \dot{e}_{ij} \;\approx\; \underbrace{\alpha}_{\text{gain}}\, d_{ij}(t) \;-\; \frac{e_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}}, \label{eq:7.4}
\end{align}
a leaky integrator driven by the pre--post coincidence $d_{ij}$ and decaying with time constant $\tau_{leak}$. This is, term by term, the canonical eligibility-trace equation of reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity \cite{izhikevich2007solving, florian2007reinforcement}: the coincidence drive plays the role of the \abbrev{STDP} kernel and the trap-discharge time $\tau_{leak}$ that of the trace time constant. The device trace is therefore not merely analogous to an eligibility trace. Under the Chapter~5 model it is one, set by coincidence-driven charge trapping and \emph{decaying} by trap discharge through $R_{leak}$, the same two physical processes that appear in Chapter~5 as the transient's rise and relaxation.\\

\noindent The learning rule is thus not merely implemented in hardware but emerges from the intrinsic dynamics of the material: the eligibility trace is the device's own trap relaxation, not an abstract variable computed alongside it. Figure~\ref{fig:7s} traces this at the electron level, from coincidence-driven trap capture, through the trapped-electron population that is the eligibility, to the reward that samples whatever survives at $t_R$.\\

\noindent This places the construction at the strong end of the biorealism spectrum of Section~\ref{subsec:learning-paradigms}, because the eligibility trace is the computational face of \emph{synaptic tagging and capture} \cite{frey1997synaptic, redondo2011making}: a coincidence sets a transient \emph{tag} that decays on its own, and a delayed neuromodulator captures it into a lasting change only while the tag survives \cite{frey1997synaptic, moncada2007induction}. What this mechanism insists on, and algorithmic models leave abstract, is that the tag be a physical, transient state with its own decay time, precisely what the device relaxation supplies. The window is measured at real synapses. Cortical synapses carry distinct potentiation and depression traces \cite{he2015eligibility}, and dopamine reinforces a recently stimulated spine only within roughly $0.3$--$2$\,s \cite{yagishita2014timewindow, gerstner2018eligibility}.\\

\noindent The correspondence is to the logic of tagging and capture, a decaying tag whose lifetime sets a capture window, and is dynamical rather than chemical. Trap emission through $R_{leak}$ is not the synapse's calcium- and protein-based machinery. The claim is therefore the weaker one that both are activity-set relaxation processes on the same behavioural timescale, not the minutes-to-hours chemistry of classical tagging and capture. Figure~\ref{fig:7v} sets the two sequences side by side.\\

\noindent That the trace is the device's own physics, not an equation imposed on it, rests on each term of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} mapping to an identified mechanism rather than a free fit. Conduction in this silicon-rich oxide is filamentary, along the percolation paths imaged by conductive atomic-force microscopy \cite{buckwell2015conductance}, proceeding by inelastic trap-assisted tunnelling between defect sites. The drive term $\alpha\,p^{(m)}(V_{max}-|v|)$ is the sequential filling of that conducting trap chain. Its compressed-exponential rise ($\beta_{fill}\!\approx\!2$, $k\!\approx\!3$) is the Erlang-$k$ waiting-time statistics of $k$ sequential first-order capture steps \cite{jimenez2001physical, vankampen2007stochastic}. The measured exponent is bias-independent ($\beta_{fill} = 1.95 \pm 0.30$, Chapter~5), which is why the cascade shape is fixed while only the rate $\alpha = k/\tau_r$ tracks bias, in the Poole--Frenkel form $\alpha(V) \propto \exp(\gamma\sqrt{V})$ expected for field-assisted trap emission \cite{frenkel1938pre}.\\

\noindent The leak term $-v/\tau_{leak}$ is the discharge of those same traps once the coincidence drive stops. Because the traps span a distribution of release energies rather than a single rate, the discharge is \emph{dispersive}, a stretched exponential ($\beta_{leak} < 1$): the Scher--Montroll signature of relaxation over a spread of trap depths \cite{scher1975anomalous}, and the mirror image of the compressed sequential fill.\\

\noindent The space-charge branch of Equation~\ref{eq:7.2}, which Chapter~5 separates from this discharge, is the drift of a mobile positive species (identified as hydrogen on this platform, at concentrations reported to modify SiO$_2$ conductivity \cite{cox2024hydrogen, vanheusden1999non}) that screens the interfacial field but carries no appreciable current. Only the trap discharge, not this slower proton decay $\tau_d$, sets the credit window $\tau_{leak}$, so the eligibility mechanism is decoupled from the identity of the mobile species. Whichever positive species drives the screening (Section~\ref{sec:5p2}) fixes the origin of $\tau_d$, but the trace is the electronic trap discharge through $R_{leak}$. Every coefficient of the eligibility ODE is therefore a measured material property, not a tuned hyperparameter.\\

\noindent This dispersive picture also yields the eligibility trace from first principles, and shows the canonical exponential trace to be a limiting case of it. Take the trace to be the population of electrons still held in the conducting traps a time $t$ after a coincidence empties charge into them at $t=0$. A single trap of release-energy $E$ emits to the conduction band at a thermally-activated first-order rate
\begin{align}
    r(E) = \nu_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{E}{k_B T}\right), \label{eq:7.5}
\end{align}
with attempt frequency $\nu_0$, so its occupancy decays as $e^{-r(E)t}$. The amorphous oxide does not present one depth but a distribution $g(E)$ of trap energies, the very dispersion that gives the stretched discharge, so the measurable eligibility is the ensemble average over that distribution,
\begin{align}
    e(t) = \int_0^{\infty} g(E)\, \exp\!\big(-r(E)\,t\big)\, dE. \label{eq:7.6}
\end{align}
For the exponential band tail $g(E) \propto \exp(-E/E_0)$ characteristic of disordered silicon-rich oxides, with disorder width $E_0$, this multiple-trapping integral is the Scher--Montroll problem \cite{scher1975anomalous, kakalios1987stretched}: its long-time form is the stretched (dispersive) exponential
\begin{align}
    e(t) \approx \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{t}{\tau_{leak}}\right)^{\beta_{leak}}\right], \qquad \beta_{leak} = \frac{k_B T}{E_0} < 1, \label{eq:7.7}
\end{align}
whose exponent $\beta_{leak}$ is fixed by the disorder width $E_0$ relative to the thermal energy and whose scale $\tau_{leak}$ is the trap-discharge retention. Equation~\ref{eq:7.7} is exactly the measured \abbrev{ITO} discharge of Chapter~5 (held-bias $\beta_{leak} = 0.54 \pm 0.17$). In the narrow-distribution limit $E_0 \to 0$ (a single trap depth) the exponent $\beta_{leak} \to 1$ and Equation~\ref{eq:7.7} collapses to the mono-exponential $e(t) = e^{-t/\tau_{leak}}$, precisely the leaky-integrator decay of the canonical eligibility trace, Equation~\ref{eq:7.4}. The trace operates field-free, where Chapter~5 shows $\beta_{leak}$ rises towards a near-single-rate $\approx0.85$. The device therefore sits close to this canonical limit, and the single-$\tau_{leak}$ surrogate of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} used in the network experiments is the correct operating-regime form rather than an idealisation discarding the dispersion. Section~\ref{sec:7p3p3} confirms this directly, the network learning the same policy at the dispersed and single-rate exponents.\\

\noindent Two features make the correspondence physical rather than nominal. First, $\tau_{leak}$ is not an arbitrary fitting knob but the measured trap-discharge relaxation time of Chapter~5. The device's slow dynamics, a liability for the static inference of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}, are by physical coincidence the seconds-scale timescale behavioural credit assignment requires. Second, the full cascade ($k>1$) departs from the idealised trace measurably: its rise is sigmoidal ($\beta_{fill}\!\approx\!2$, $k\!\approx\!3$) rather than instantaneous, a short latency whose cost is quantified in Section~\ref{sec:7p3}. Because each state is first-order, the cascade discretises by forward Euler into a stateful per-synapse update that runs inside the network time loop at no extra modelling cost \cite{zhou2022memprop, neftci2019surrogate, eshraghian2021training}.\\

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7a} shows the resulting trace for three retention settings: a sigmoidal rise of seconds followed by a relaxation whose duration is set by $\tau_{leak}$. Two elements are imposed by the surrounding circuit rather than intrinsic to the device. The coincidence drive $d_{ij}(t)$ and the reward gating below are encodings placed around a two-terminal device that does not itself compute coincidence or sense reward; the device supplies the \emph{trace dynamics}, the network the \emph{three-factor structure}.\\

\noindent The timescales make the case for biorealism quantitative, and Figure~\ref{fig:7i} sets them side by side. The gold device's \emph{measured} dynamics span the fitted rise $\tau_r \approx 1.9$--$14.5$\,s and space-charge decay $\tau_d \approx 36$--$537$\,s (Chapter~5, Table~\ref{table:5c}). The trap discharge defining $\tau_{leak}$ is measured at $\sim$1.3\,s held-bias on the \abbrev{ITO} devices, extrapolating to a field-free retention of a few seconds (Equation~\ref{eq:5.11}). The biological eligibility window spans $\sim$0.3--10\,s \cite{yagishita2014timewindow, he2015eligibility} and sits within the device's measured band, so its natural retention overlaps biology without rescaling.\\

\noindent The retention values used in the learning experiments, $\tau_{leak} = 1$, $5$, $10$, and $20$\,s, are settings of the $R_{leak}C$ knob rather than separately measured constants. The $5$--$20$\,s values lie within the measured dynamical band. The $1$\,s setting sits just below the slowest measured rise and is the one point leaning on the knob beyond what these devices were measured to do (Figure~\ref{fig:7i}).

\subsection[The Three-Factor Learning Rule]{The Three-Factor Learning Rule}
\label{sec:7p1p2}

\noindent The device trace serves as the eligibility term $e_{ij}(t)$ of a three-factor rule. A pre--post coincidence drives the device state; a delayed global reward then gates the surviving trace into a weight change,
\begin{align}
    \Delta w_{ij} = \eta \left( R - b \right) e_{ij}(t_R), \label{eq:7.8}
\end{align}
where $t_R$ is the reward time, $R$ the reward on the trial, $b$ a slowly adapting reward baseline (an advantage estimate), and $\eta$ the learning rate. The reward enters as a global scalar setting both the sign and magnitude of plasticity. On under-reward ($R<b$) the active synapses are depressed \cite{fremaux2016neuromodulated, florian2007reinforcement}.\\

\noindent A selective trace alone does not yield selective weights: reward-uncorrelated synapses that happen to be active also accumulate eligibility, and without a counterbalancing term every weight drifts together. Following the canonical resolution of the distal-reward problem \cite{izhikevich2007solving}, the coincidence kernel driving the device state is made \emph{leak-dominant} (signed): a causal pre-then-post coincidence contributes positively, while presynaptic activity that does not drive the postsynaptic neuron contributes a smaller negative term,
\begin{align}
    d_{ij}(t) = x^{pre}_i(t) \left[ \mathbf{1}\!\left( x^{post}_j(t) \right) - \lambda_d \left( 1 - \mathbf{1}\!\left( x^{post}_j(t) \right) \right) \right], \label{eq:7.9}
\end{align}
where $x^{pre}_i$ and $x^{post}_j$ are the pre- and postsynaptic spike trains, $\mathbf{1}(\cdot)$ indicates a postsynaptic spike in the time step, and $\lambda_d \in (0,1)$ sets the acausal-depression weight. A reward-correlated cue produces mostly causal coincidences and a net-positive trace. An uncorrelated distractor produces mostly acausal activity and a net-negative trace that decays rather than drifting up. This signed eligibility, rather than weight normalisation, makes the rewarded synapse win.\\

\noindent In the spiking network the postsynaptic neuron follows the \abbrev{LIF} unit of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} (Equations~\ref{eq:6.1}--\ref{eq:6.2}), here with an explicit leakage and an added membrane-noise term,
\begin{align}
    V^{m}_j(t + \Delta t) = V^{m}_j(t) + \sum_i w_{ij}\, x^{pre}_i(t) - \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_m} V^{m}_j(t) + \sigma\, \xi_j(t), \label{eq:7.10}
\end{align}
emitting a spike and resetting when $V^{m}_j \ge V_{th}$. Here $\xi_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ is per-neuron noise of scale $\sigma$ providing exploration, and $\tau_m$ is the membrane time constant. The architecture and per-synapse mechanism are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:7b}.

\section[Closed-Loop Reinforcement Learning]{Closed-Loop Reinforcement Learning}
\label{sec:7p2}

\noindent The rule is evaluated first in a reduced trace-level model isolating the credit-assignment function, then in a full \abbrev{LIF} spiking network in which the device state is integrated online from real spike coincidences, and finally in a closed reinforcement-learning loop. All three use the canonical distal-reward task. A cue synapse undergoes a coincidence, a reward is delivered after an action--reward delay $D$ with interleaved unrewarded catch trials, and reward-uncorrelated distractor synapses are active throughout. A correct learner raises the cue weight and leaves the distractors near baseline despite the delay. Success is measured as in the established literature \cite{izhikevich2007solving, legenstein2008learning}: at the trace level by the cue-to-distractor weight ratio, and in the spiking network by saturation of the cue synapse toward its conductance bound while distractors stay at baseline.

\subsection[Retention as a Credit-Assignment Window]{Retention as a Credit-Assignment Window}
\label{sec:7p2p1}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/credit_window_vs_retention.png}}
    \caption[Retention tunes the credit-assignment window.]{Retention tunes the credit-assignment window at the trace level: learned cue-to-distractor weight ratio against action--reward delay $D$ (mean of 20 seeds, shaded 95\% confidence interval), for three retention times and a no-trace control. The dotted line marks the learned threshold.}
    \label{fig:7c}
\end{figure}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7c} reports the trace-level result. The no-trace control never learns (ratio at unity for all delays), confirming the trace is necessary. The device trace assigns credit, and the maximum learnable delay scales monotonically with retention: $\tau_{leak} = 1$, $5$, and $20$\,s learn delays up to roughly $5$, $40$, and $100$\,s respectively. The long-retention trace is, as expected, weaker at the shortest delays, because its sigmoidal rise has not yet peaked when a near-immediate reward arrives. Retention therefore trades short-delay against long-delay performance, a tunable device property rather than a fixed kernel. This short-delay weakness is the visible edge of a more general property, examined next: the rise makes the trace \emph{peaked} rather than monotone, so it does not merely bridge a delay but selects a preferred one.\\

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/interval_selective_eligibility.png}}
    \caption[The dual-phase trace is a tunable interval-selective eligibility.]{The dual-phase trace is a tunable interval filter, not a recency-weighted memory. (a) The device eligibility of Equations~\ref{eq:7.1}--\ref{eq:7.3} (normalised, $\tau_{leak}=10$\,s) is \emph{peaked} at a non-zero lag $t^\ast$, whereas the canonical exponential trace is monotone and maximal at lag zero. (b) Learned timing selectivity $S = w_{\rm pref}/w_{\rm late}$ at the trace level (20 seeds); $S>1$ means the trace credited the preferred interval over the reward-time coincidence. The preferred interval slides rightward as $\tau_{leak}$ lengthens.}
    \label{fig:7o}
\end{figure}

\noindent This peakedness is a qualitative difference from the canonical trace, with a behavioural consequence so far left implicit. The exponential trace of reward-modulated \abbrev{STDP} is monotone, largest at lag zero and decaying thereafter. It is \emph{recency}-weighted, crediting whatever coincided just before the reward. The device trace, built from the trap-cascade rise of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1}, instead peaks at a non-zero lag $t^\ast$ and falls away on both sides (Figure~\ref{fig:7o}a). It is a band-pass in time: it credits a coincidence at its \emph{preferred interval} and suppresses one at lag zero.\\

\noindent To test whether this changes what is learned, two synapses were placed in competition at the trace level, both perfectly reward-correlated so that only their timing differed. One fired at a chosen interval $D$ before the reward, the other at a near-zero lag, the reward-time confound recency weighting cannot reject. At the design point $D = t^\ast$, the device trace learned a selectivity of $S = w_{\rm pref}/w_{\rm late} = 3.96$ ($95\%$ confidence interval $[3.95, 3.96]$ over 20 seeds), crediting the preferred interval roughly fourfold over the reward-time coincidence. A decay-matched exponential trace gave $S = 0.48$ ($[0.48, 0.49]$), crediting the lag-zero confound more, the two intervals disjoint. Sweeping retention shifted the preferred interval: short retention was most selective at short $D$, long retention at long $D$, the peak of $S(D)$ sliding rightward with $\tau_{leak}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:7o}b).\\

\noindent The device thus supplies a \emph{tunable} interval-selective eligibility that a single fixed exponential cannot, and that matches biology more closely than the idealised trace: the dopamine eligibility window is itself peaked at a short non-zero delay rather than at coincidence \cite{yagishita2014timewindow}. A concurrent algorithmic proposal, cascading eligibility traces, likewise builds a delay-peaked trace from a cascade of states \cite{ralambomihanta2025cascading}. There the cascade is a designed state-space model, whereas here it is the measured trap-filling kinetics ($\beta_{fill}\approx2$) of a fabricated oxide, the peak emergent rather than engineered. The short-delay weakness above is therefore the cost side of that capability, not only a limitation.\\

\noindent To confirm the mechanism is not an artefact of the trace-level reduction, the device state is embedded as an online per-synapse trace in the spiking network of Figure~\ref{fig:7b}(a). Here $N=8$ Poisson inputs project through plastic synapses to one \abbrev{LIF} output neuron, each synapse integrating the leak-dominant coincidence kernel through the fitted device dynamics at every time step. Because the device relaxes on a seconds timescale, it integrates the coincidence \emph{rate} rather than resolving millisecond spike pairs. The action it marks is a sustained epoch of correlated firing, the regime where a slow trace is the appropriate primitive.\\

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7d} reports the outcome by the saturation measure standard in this literature \cite{izhikevich2007solving}. With long retention ($\tau_{leak} = 10$\,s) the rewarded cue synapse is driven to its conductance bound across action--reward delays of $\sim$5--20\,s while the seven distractor synapses remain at baseline, the near-total separation that defines successful distal-reward learning. Shorter retention ($\tau_{leak} = 2$ and $0.5$\,s) fails to bridge these delays, reproducing the retention-tunable window of the trace level, and the dip at the shortest delay again reflects the slow sigmoidal rise.\\

\noindent The preceding tasks fix the reward schedule externally. To close the loop, the reward is made contingent on the network's own action, realising the contextual bandit of Section~\ref{subsec:learning-paradigms} in spiking hardware. A stimulus selects the state. Two \abbrev{LIF} action neurons compete and the one that spikes more is taken, the stochastic spiking of Equation~\ref{eq:7.10} providing exploration. The reward, after delay $D$, is given only when the chosen action matches the rewarding action for that state. The four plastic synapses carry the device trace, updated at reward by the signed rule of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8}.\\

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7e} shows the learning curve. With the device trace the reward rate climbs from chance to near unity ($0.99$, 95\% confidence interval $[0.98, 0.99]$ over 20 seeds) within roughly one hundred trials, while the no-trace control remains at chance ($0.51$, $[0.48, 0.54]$). The retention dependence carries over. Long retention ($\tau_{leak} = 10$\,s) learns the policy across delays of $1$--$20$\,s, whereas shorter retention ($\tau_{leak} = 2$\,s) succeeds only at short delays and falls to chance by $D = 20$\,s. The device trace therefore not only assigns credit but closes a reinforcement-learning loop, its retention time setting the action--reward delay over which a policy can be learned.

\subsection[Sequential Credit Assignment and a Benchmark]{Sequential Credit Assignment and a Benchmark}
\label{sec:7p2p3}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/sequential_credit_benchmark.png}}
    \caption[Sequential credit assignment and an algorithmic benchmark.]{Sequential credit assignment on the T-maze (20 seeds, 95\% confidence intervals): reward-rate learning curves for the device trace, the R-STDP and e-prop baselines, and the no-trace control, against chance ($0.5$) and the $0.75$ criterion. All three trace mechanisms reach ceiling while the control stays at chance; the retention dependence of the maximum learnable delay is quantified in Figure~\ref{fig:7l}.}
    \label{fig:7j}
\end{figure}

\noindent The contextual bandit is a single step. It isolates the credit-assignment mechanism but leaves open whether the trace can bridge a reward across a \emph{trajectory}, where the cue determining the correct action and the moment it is taken are separated by intervening steps, exactly the regime an eligibility trace exists for.\\

\noindent The device trace was therefore embedded in a sequential T-maze: a start cue sets which of two arms is rewarded, a three-state stem follows, and at the junction the agent must route to the cued arm, with reward delivered only at the goal after a further delay $D$. The stem is a one-way corridor traversed regardless of action, so the only learned decision is the cue-conditioned arm choice, taken a full trajectory plus delay $D$ after the cue. Because the eligibility gate integrates continuously and decays across this interval, bridging cue to choice requires a retention long enough to span it. The learned object is a policy, not a fixed stimulus--reward association.\\

\noindent With retention long enough to span the trajectory, the device trace learns the task, reaching a reward rate of $1.00$ (95\% confidence interval $[1.00, 1.00]$ over 20 seeds) against chance $0.5$ and the $0.75$ criterion, while the no-trace control stays at chance ($0.51$, $[0.49, 0.52]$). The necessity of the trace therefore survives the move from one step to a trajectory (Figure~\ref{fig:7j}). Scoring the learned weights directly, the agent recovers the correct cue-conditioned action in $0.86$ of states ($[0.79, 0.93]$, well above the $0.75$ criterion), confirming a policy rather than a single association is learned. The shortfall from unity reflects a minority of seeds that settle on one arm under one cue context.\\

\noindent The maximum learnable goal delay widens with retention, the sequential analogue of the trace-level delay window of Section~\ref{sec:7p2p1} and the device-physics origin of the scaling relation, which Section~\ref{sec:7p3p3} quantifies on a dense retention sweep (overall slope $s_D\approx15$, rising to a plateau of $\approx16$ at long retention; Figure~\ref{fig:7l}).\\

\noindent On the same task, network, seeds, and budget, the device trace was benchmarked against the two established algorithmic eligibility traces of spiking reinforcement learning. The first is reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity (R-STDP), whose eligibility is the canonical \emph{abstract exponential} trace of prior work \cite{florian2007reinforcement, izhikevich2007solving, legenstein2008learning}, matched here in decay timescale to $\tau_{leak}$. The second is e-prop \cite{bellec2020solution}, whose eligibility is instead \emph{computed} from the network's own dynamics (the pseudo-derivative of the membrane potential times a filtered presynaptic trace) as a biologically plausible approximation to backpropagation through time, here gated by a policy-gradient learning signal over a softmax action layer.\\

\noindent At an operating point where retention spans the trajectory, all three trace mechanisms reach ceiling and the no-trace control stays at chance (Table~\ref{table:7a}, Figure~\ref{fig:7j}). They differ not in accuracy but in how the eligibility is \emph{formed}. e-prop computes it from network dynamics, at the cost of a per-neuron eligibility and a separate learning-signal pathway. R-STDP hand-sets it as an exponential kernel with a chosen decay constant. The device supplies it physically, as a trap-discharge relaxation that is free, local, and fabrication-set ($\tau_{leak} = R_{leak}C$) rather than hand-tuned. That a fixed-form physical relaxation matches both a computed and a hand-set algorithmic trace is the point: the device is a competent substitute for the eligibility traces of established spiking-RL rules, distinguished by a retention engineered in silicon rather than specified as a hyperparameter.\\

\noindent The scope of the e-prop comparison is stated plainly. The implementation here is the shallow, single-decision-layer reduction of e-prop. It is faithful to the per-synapse computed eligibility and the policy-gradient learning signal, and robust across the learning rate rather than a knife-edge. The feedforward T-maze does not, however, exercise e-prop's recurrent temporal-credit machinery, its principal advantage on tasks with hidden recurrent state. The claim is therefore the bounded one that all three eligibility mechanisms solve this trajectory-bridging task at ceiling, not that the device trace approximates a gradient.

\section[Scaling and Hardware Realisation]{Scaling and Hardware Realisation}
\label{sec:7p3}

\noindent The closed-loop demonstration uses a minimal two-action network. This section asks how far the primitive reaches: how it scales as the state and action spaces grow, how it separates the cost of the learning rule from that of the device, and how it carries credit through depth as well as time. It then turns to biological grounding: the behavioural signature of dopaminergic learning on a canonical human task, then the capstone in which the whole stack learns under a measured dopamine reward, decoded from a public mouse photometry dataset rather than recorded here.

\subsection[Crossbar Realisation and Scaling to Depth]{Crossbar Realisation and Scaling to Depth}
\label{sec:7p3p1}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/closed_loop_crossbar.png}}
    \caption[Crossbar realisation of the closed-loop task.]{Crossbar realisation of the closed-loop task. (a) The contextual bandit mapped onto an $S \times A$ memristive array, with a single global third-factor line broadcasting $(R-b)$ to every crosspoint. (b) One crosspoint: a differential pair encoding the signed weight, the device transient supplying the local eligibility trace, and the reward-gated local update.}
    \label{fig:7f}
\end{figure}

\noindent The mechanism maps directly onto a memristive crossbar (Figure~\ref{fig:7f}). State inputs drive the array rows, and the action neurons read the bitline currents, the analogue vector--matrix product of Equations~\ref{eq:6.17}--\ref{eq:6.18}. Each signed weight $w_{ij}$ is encoded as a differential conductance pair $(G^{+}_{ij}, G^{-}_{ij})$ on two bitlines (Equation~\ref{eq:6.28}), so the current read by action neuron $j$ when the state line carries the encoded input voltage $V^{in}_i = k_V x^{pre}_i$ (with $k_V$ a fixed spike-to-voltage gain) is
\begin{align}
    I_j = \sum_i \left( G^{+}_{ij} - G^{-}_{ij} \right) V^{in}_i, \qquad w_{ij} \propto G^{+}_{ij} - G^{-}_{ij}, \label{eq:7.11}
\end{align}
Kirchhoff summation along the column, the same in-memory product used for inference in Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}. The novelty is that each crosspoint carries, beyond its programmed weight, the \emph{dynamical} state of Equations~\ref{eq:7.1}--\ref{eq:7.3}. The trap-cascade variables $v^{(m)}_{ij}$ evolve locally from the coincidence drive, so the eligibility trace $e_{ij}$ is computed where it is stored, with no separate memory.\\

\noindent The weight update is likewise local. Discretising the three-factor rule of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8} over a programming step $\Delta t_p$ gives a conductance increment
\begin{align}
    \Delta G^{\pm}_{ij} = \pm\, \eta\, \big( R - b \big)\, e_{ij}(t_R), \label{eq:7.12}
\end{align}
applied as complementary potentiation/depression pulses to the two bitlines. Every term on the right is local or on one shared line: $e_{ij}(t_R)$ is the local device state, and the scalar advantage $(R-b)$ is broadcast to the array on one global wire, encoding the third (neuromodulatory) factor. The update therefore needs neither a transposed weight matrix nor a per-synapse error signal, the two operations that make backpropagation expensive in hardware \cite{bellec2020solution, zhou2022memprop}. Its cost scales with array size alone. Because each cascade state is first-order, its forward-Euler step
\begin{align}
    v^{(m)}_{ij}(t{+}\Delta t) = v^{(m)}_{ij}(t) + \Delta t \left[ \alpha\, p^{(m)}_{ij} \big( V_{max} - |v^{(m)}_{ij}| \big) - \frac{v^{(m)}_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}} \right] \label{eq:7.13}
\end{align}
is an in-place accumulation a compact peripheral or in-situ relaxation circuit can perform without leaving the crosspoint, just as the device's own $R_{leak}C$ relaxation realises the $-v/\tau_{leak}$ term physically rather than arithmetically.\\

\noindent Two implementation costs are specific to the two-terminal device. First, delivering the reward factor $(R-b)$ requires gating the programming pulse of Equation~\ref{eq:7.12} by a global signal, which a 1T1R cell or shared row/column driver supplies but a three-terminal synaptic transistor would provide natively through its gate, trading the two-terminal cell's density against cleaner third-factor routing. Second, the eligibility must persist between coincidence and reward, which the measured $\tau_{leak}$ provides over its seconds-scale window; longer delays would need a longer-retention variant or a refreshed capacitive hold. Both are circuit-design choices rather than obstacles to the rule, which is agnostic to how $e_{ij}$ and $(R-b)$ are physically realised.\\

\noindent A cheap update is not the same as a learnable policy. Generalising the bandit to an $S \times A$ array separates the two (Figure~\ref{fig:7g}a). The device trace is necessary and learns at every size tested, above chance while the no-trace control stays at chance. Within a fixed budget, however, it reaches the convergence criterion only up to about four actions, with trials-to-criterion growing steeply ($\sim$290, $\sim$460, and $\sim$820 trials for two, three, and four actions). This is the expected sample-efficiency cost of a larger action space under the undirected (membrane-noise) exploration used here, where a random action is rewarded with probability $1/A$.\\

\noindent Standard remedies remove the wall (Figure~\ref{fig:7g}b). Holding the device gate and learning rule fixed on the failing $8\times8$ case, quadrupling the budget restores convergence (from $0.31$ to $0.70$), and adding directed (annealed) exploration reaches $0.97$. Directed exploration alone at the original budget is insufficient. Substituting an abstract exponential trace for the device at the original budget also clears the criterion ($0.75$) where the device does not, confirming the wall is one of samples rather than of the rule. This also exposes a genuine device cost: the slow sigmoidal rise has not peaked when a near-immediate reward arrives (the low-delay dip of Figures~\ref{fig:7c}--\ref{fig:7d}), a real but surmountable sample-efficiency penalty. Reaching large action spaces thus calls for the usual reinforcement-learning machinery, directed exploration, reward shaping, hierarchy, or function approximation across states, layered on this primitive rather than replacing it.\\

\noindent The last of those remedies, function approximation across states, can be supplied without violating the device timescale, resolving the scaling wall rather than sidestepping it. The construction is a hybrid network (Figure~\ref{fig:7h}) dividing labour by timescale. A convolutional spiking perception front-end, trained by backpropagation through time as in Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}, classifies a high-dimensional noisy stimulus, $256$-pixel oriented gratings at four orientations, reaching $86.7\%$ accuracy, and is frozen. Its low-dimensional class readout becomes the state seen by the shallow device-eligibility decision layer, on which the same signed three-factor bandit of Section~\ref{sec:7p2p1} runs. The slow transient is thus confined to the decision layer where the action--reward loop lives, rather than threaded through a network whose millisecond inference its seconds-scale dynamics could not match. The gradient-trained stage does the perceptual work the eligibility rule cannot, the device the credit assignment the front-end is not wired for.\\

\noindent Three conditions were compared at an equal reinforcement-learning budget, with criteria fixed before the run as throughout this chapter (Table~\ref{table:7c}). With the front-end the decision layer learns the policy, reaching the $0.625$ criterion, whereas the same rule and device trace on the raw pixels, and a no-trace control with the full hybrid wiring but the device eligibility zeroed, both stay at chance. The two controls answer separate questions. The raw-pixel control shows the bottleneck is perceptual rather than a limitation of the rule, the $+0.40$ gap to the hybrid being the front-end's contribution. The no-trace control shows the eligibility primitive remains necessary even with the front-end in place, since removing it collapses the policy despite the learned features. The perception stage therefore enables the device primitive on high-dimensional input but does not replace it.\\

\noindent The same hybrid stack also holds at array scale under the measured device-fault prior (Figure~\ref{fig:7hs}). Sweeping the hidden width $H$ and the stuck-device fraction behind the real frozen front-end, the full local stack (trace, feedback alignment, and homeostasis) reaches $0.48$--$0.56$ across $H=32$--$512$ and stuck fractions up to $50\%$, disjoint throughout from the eligibility-zeroed control at chance ($1/A=0.25$). \noindent The hybrid agent confines the device trace to a shallow decision layer behind a separately-trained front-end. A sharper question is whether the trace can instead train a network of more than one plastic layer entirely locally, carrying credit through depth as well as time. The experiment establishes that the deep agent learns but unreliably, then diagnoses the cause and removes it with a local mechanism already developed in this thesis, ending in a reliable deep all-local learner within bounded scope.\\

\noindent The task is an exclusive-or (XOR) contextual bandit: two binary cue features, the rewarded action their XOR, encoded so the four cue patterns carry equal total input drive. XOR is not linearly separable, so a single trained layer cannot represent the policy \cite{rumelhart1986learning} and the task requires a trained hidden layer, the minimal honest test of depth. The network is two layers of signed device synapses (four input lines to a hidden layer of leaky integrate-and-fire neurons to two action neurons) with the trap-cascade eligibility of Equations~\ref{eq:7.1}--\ref{eq:7.3} on both weight matrices, so the temporal factor is the device trace throughout. What varies between conditions is only the \emph{spatial}-credit pathway, how each layer learns which of its synapses to change.\\

\noindent The global-scalar rule broadcasts the same advantage $(R-b)$ of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8} to every synapse in both layers, as in the single-layer tasks. Direct feedback alignment (\abbrevdef{DFA}) instead gives the hidden layer a \emph{per-neuron} learning signal, projecting the output error through a fixed random feedback matrix \cite{lillicrap2016random}. Because that matrix is fixed and independent of the forward weights, the update needs no weight transport and stays as local and crossbar-compatible as the global rule. Three controls bound the result: a single trained layer (shallow), a fixed-random hidden layer with only the output trained (an extreme-learning-machine, or \abbrevdef{ELM}, measuring what a random projection alone buys), and a no-trace control.\\

\noindent The pre-registered predictions are largely borne out (Figure~\ref{fig:7m}). The shallow network cannot represent XOR and sits below chance ($0.31$, $95\%$ confidence interval $[0.28, 0.35]$ over $20$ seeds), confirming the task demands depth. The no-trace control fails ($0.54$, $[0.47, 0.61]$), confirming the eligibility is still necessary. The random-feature \abbrev{ELM} does not solve the task ($0.50$, $[0.40, 0.61]$).\\

\noindent The decisive comparison is between the two deep, fully-trained rules. The pure global scalar stays at chance ($0.50$, $[0.49, 0.52]$): a single broadcast reward cannot tell the hidden layer which of its units helped, the structural-credit problem that is the depth analogue of the action-space wall above. Direct feedback alignment, with its per-neuron signal, is the only rule to learn, reaching $0.73$ ($[0.60, 0.86]$), a margin over the global scalar whose confidence intervals are disjoint. Eleven of the twenty seeds solve XOR exactly, at $1.00$. A physical eligibility trace can therefore carry credit through a trained hidden layer, fully locally, on a task no single layer and no global-scalar rule can solve.\\

\noindent As first run the result was a possibility rather than a reliable method. It did not reach the criterion on average ($0.73<0.75$), performance being sharply bimodal, the solving seeds at $1.00$ and the rest near chance. Two natural explanations are ruled out. It is not a feedback-alignment failure (the forward and feedback matrices stay near orthogonal for solving and failing seeds alike), nor a failure of hidden-layer learning (the hidden weights move just as far in failures as in successes). The failing seeds instead \emph{collapse} onto a constant policy, locking onto a noise-favoured action before the annealed exploration could discover that the other action is needed for half the cues.\\

\noindent This is an exploration failure, the same one met in the action-space scaling above. The bottleneck is neither the device trace nor the per-layer credit, both of which work, but the undirected exploration of the closed loop, and raising the exploration floor barely helps. Making that exploration robust is an open problem under the all-local constraint. The constraint permits each synapse only its own eligibility trace and a single broadcast scalar, yet every effective directed-exploration method needs non-local information, a visitation count, a learned predictor, an ensemble disagreement, or a policy-entropy gradient, each re-importing the per-state memory or weight transport the crossbar-native rule was built to avoid. The collapse is moreover a self-reinforcing attractor: the eligibility-gated update strengthens the prematurely-chosen action while no local signal reports the vanishing policy entropy a global critic would catch. A \emph{direct} attack on the exploration problem is therefore blocked. The collapse it produces, however, admits a weaker local remedy.\\

    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/deep_all_local_crossbar.png}}
    \caption[Crossbar realisation of the deep all-local agent.]{Crossbar realisation of the deep all-local agent, extending the single-layer mapping of Figure~\ref{fig:7f}. (a) Two stacked memristive arrays ($W_1$, $W_2$) flank a hidden leaky integrate-and-fire layer, with the device eligibility at every crosspoint and the reward $(R-b)$ broadcast on one wire. A \emph{fixed random} feedback array (purple) projects the output signal back, $L_h = B L_a$, giving per-neuron credit with no weight transport, and a \emph{local homeostatic} regulator (orange) holds each hidden neuron's firing rate near a shared target. (b) One hidden crosspoint, as the single-layer cell of Figure~\ref{fig:7f} but with the local update summing the feedback-aligned credit (purple) and the homeostatic term (orange) of Equation~\ref{eq:7.14}.}
    \label{fig:7n}
\end{figure}

\noindent That remedy prevents the collapse rather than directing the exploration. The self-reinforcing slide onto a constant action has a purely local signature: a hidden unit that locks the network into one action does so by firing far above its peers, an imbalance each neuron can sense in its own activity with no global information. This is exactly the quantity the homeostatic plasticity of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} regulates. A local homeostatic term is therefore added to the hidden layer, so each neuron keeps a slow estimate of its own firing rate and nudges its incoming weights to hold that rate near a shared target. This opposes the winner-take-all collapse while leaving the credit-assignment signal untouched (Figure~\ref{fig:7n}).\\

\noindent The mechanism is principled, not a tuned correction. The correct exclusive-or policy has balanced marginal activity across the two actions, so regulating hidden activity toward balance resists the collapse without resisting the solution. It is also local, a per-row activity integrator rather than a global computation. The hidden-layer update of Figure~\ref{fig:7n}(b) is then the sum of two local terms,
\begin{align}
    \Delta w_{ij} = \underbrace{\eta\,(R-b)\,[B\mathbf{L}]_j\, e_{ij}(t_R)}_{\text{per-neuron credit (\abbrev{DFA})}} \;+\; \underbrace{\eta_h\,(\rho^{\ast} - \bar{\rho}_j)\, w_{ij}}_{\text{local homeostasis}}, \label{eq:7.14}
\end{align}
where $[B\mathbf{L}]_j$ is the fixed-random feedback projection of the output learning signal $\mathbf{L}$ onto hidden neuron $j$ (the per-neuron credit the global scalar lacks), $\bar{\rho}_j$ that neuron's slow running estimate of its own firing rate, $\rho^{\ast}$ the shared target rate, and $\eta_h$ the homeostatic rate. Every quantity is local, so the update needs neither a transposed weight matrix nor a per-synapse gradient.\\

\noindent This set-point term is not an added heuristic but the linearised transient of the device's own fill--emission balance. The trap occupancy of Chapter~\ref{ch:model} (Equation~\ref{eq:5.12}, $\dot v = f(V_{max}-v) - g\,v$, with field-driven fill rate $f$ and field-free emission rate $g=1/\tau_{leak}$) has the steady state $v^{\ast} = \rho\,V_{max}$, $\rho = f/(f+g)$, the measured floor of Equation~\ref{eq:5.13}. Linearising about it,
\begin{align}
\frac{dv}{dt} = -(f+g)\,(v - v^{\ast}), \label{eq:7homeo}
\end{align}
the occupancy relaxes toward that set-point at the held-bias rate $f+g = 1/\tau_{hb}$: a negative feedback returning the trap state, and the firing rate it gates, to the fill--emission balance whenever drive pushes it away. Writing the gated weight as $\bar\rho_j = v/V_{max}$ and the balance as $\rho^{\ast}=\rho$, Equation~\ref{eq:7homeo} is the homeostatic term of Equation~\ref{eq:7.14}: the set-point, its value, and the stabilising sign are the device's, while the multiplicative $w_{ij}$ is a synaptic-scaling implementation of the drift rather than a feature of Equation~\ref{eq:7homeo}. The same balance read as a steady state, rather than a transient, is the gain gate of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} (Equation~\ref{eq:6.43}), so a single measured relaxation supplies both: the retention $\tau_{leak}$ that sets the eligibility trace also sets the homeostasis that stabilises it.\\

\noindent With this stabiliser the deep all-local agent learns reliably, reaching $1.00$ ($95\%$ confidence interval $[1.00, 1.00]$ over $20$ seeds), all twenty seeds solving exclusive-or exactly, against the $0.73$ of the unregularised rule, the two intervals disjoint (Figure~\ref{fig:7m}). That a fix targeting the diagnosed cause succeeds confirms it. The limiting factor was a stability failure, not the device trace or the per-layer credit, and the cure is the same homeostasis the device required for static inference in Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}.\\

\noindent That the credit term alone collapses and homeostasis rescues it is the predicted consequence of a long-standing principle: associative plasticity is positive feedback, and Hebbian learning has long been held to need a homeostatic counterpart to keep it from running away \cite{abbott2000taming, zenke2013homeostasis, zenke2015orchestrated}. Figure~\ref{fig:7u} draws why this holds even under the noisier single-trial reward of the capstone below. This convergence carries the chapter's strongest unifying message. The silicon-oxide transient supplies both of biology's slow, local learning primitives from one intrinsic relaxation, the eligibility being its trap discharge and the homeostasis its activity-set conductance adjustment, so a single material instantiates the two mechanisms learning theory and neurobiology treat as distinct.\\

\noindent The two primitives have so far been exercised separately, the trace bridging delay and homeostasis stabilising depth, so a final pair of experiments stresses temporal credit directly and then both primitives at once. The temporal effect is first isolated in a shallow two-action delayed-match-to-sample task \cite{miller1996neural}, the working-memory paradigm whose seconds-scale sample--test gap defines the eligibility-trace regime. A sample coincidence comes early, an uninformative distractor near reward, the decision and reward a seconds-scale gap later, so only a trace whose surviving eligibility still favours the earlier sample assigns credit correctly. The device band-pass trace does so and saturates ($1.00$), whereas a recency-weighted exponential of matched retention over-credits the near-reward distractor ($0.86$, intervals disjoint) and the no-trace control stays at chance (Figure~\ref{fig:7sdms}), the closed-loop form of the interval selectivity of Figure~\ref{fig:7o}.\\

\noindent The scope of this resolution is narrower than it may appear: homeostasis sidesteps directed exploration rather than solving it. Preventing a premature collapse needs only a local stability signal, which a neuron's own activity supplies. Steering exploration toward the informative parts of a state space, by contrast, needs the global, state-dependent uncertainty no synapse can compute. The homeostatic result therefore establishes a bounded but real claim: deep all-local reinforcement learning with a physical eligibility trace is not only possible but \emph{reliable} where the binding limitation is stability rather than exploration, achieved within the local, crossbar-native constraint and reusing for learning the mechanism the device required for inference. On a task whose difficulty lies in genuine directed exploration, such as a sparse-reward maze, the open problem above would instead bind.\\

\subsection[Grounding in Dopaminergic Learning]{Grounding in Dopaminergic Learning}
\label{sec:7p2p4}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]

(b) Test-phase choose-A and avoid-B rates for the single-layer trace: the canonical Frank asymmetry. (c) Driven by the same subjects' own real-\abbrev{EEG}-decoded reward, the single-layer agent still learns this real human paradigm ($0.64$, $[0.60, 0.69]$), above chance and above its shuffled-reward control ($0.51$, $[0.48, 0.54]$).}
    \label{fig:7q}

\noindent With capability established through depth, the chapter turns to biological grounding. The reward-modulated three-factor rule is a model of \emph{dopaminergic} learning, grounded against dopamine from two complementary directions on one underlying reward-prediction error: its behavioural signature here, and the measured neuromodulator itself in the capstone that follows. The behavioural check is whether the device trace solves an \emph{established} dopaminergic paradigm for which human and animal behaviour is documented: the probabilistic-selection task of Frank, Seeberger and O'Reilly \cite{frank2004carrot}, in which the positive- versus negative-feedback asymmetry tracks dopaminergic state. Three stimulus pairs are reinforced at fixed probabilities (AB at $80/20$, CD at $70/30$, EF at $60/40$). The agent sees one pair per trial, picks a stimulus, and is rewarded stochastically at that stimulus's probability, so the better stimulus of each pair emerges only by integrating reward over many trials, the regime an eligibility trace is built for. A test phase then presents novel recombinations without feedback and measures two diagnostic tendencies: \emph{choose-A}, selecting the most-rewarded stimulus (positive-feedback learning), and \emph{avoid-B}, avoiding the least-rewarded one (negative-feedback learning).\\

\noindent The device trace learns the task. A single trained layer reaches a higher-reinforcement-choice probability of $0.86$ ($95\%$ confidence interval $[0.84, 0.88]$ over 20 seeds) against the $0.75$ criterion, while the no-trace control stays at chance ($0.52$, $[0.45, 0.58]$). The signed three-factor rule, with the leak-dominant kernel supplying the negative term, learns from reward and omission alike (Figure~\ref{fig:7q}a). In the test phase the agent reproduces the canonical Frank signature (Figure~\ref{fig:7q}b): it both selects the most-rewarded stimulus (choose-A $0.91$, $[0.86, 0.96]$) and avoids the least-rewarded one (avoid-B $1.00$, $[1.00, 1.00]$), the positive- and negative-feedback asymmetry defining the human and animal data on this task \cite{frank2004carrot}. The comparison is kept qualitative: the agent reproduces the direction of the asymmetry rather than a quantitative fit, and avoid-B saturates at ceiling because the leak-dominant kernel's negative term makes omission maximally informative. On this paradigm the policy is linearly separable in the stimulus code, so a single trained device-synapse layer suffices.\\

\noindent The same Frank task admits a sharper, biologically-driven version of this check. Replacing the task's ground-truth reward with the reward positivity decoded from the participants' own \abbrev{EEG}, the single-layer agent still learns it ($0.64$, $[0.60, 0.69]$ over 20 seeds), above chance and above a noise-matched shuffled-reward control ($0.51$, $[0.48, 0.54]$; Figure~\ref{fig:7q}c). This settles the behavioural half of the biological question: the device behaves like a dopaminergic learner, both reproducing the feedback asymmetry and learning under a biologically-decoded reward, in this shallow, linearly-separable setting. The mechanistic half is the capstone that follows: whether the same primitive can \emph{learn from} the dopamine signal itself in the harder deep setting, where the noisy single-trial reward most stresses the stability learning depends on.\\

\noindent That capstone is the hardest test of the homeostatic stabiliser, because the noisy single-trial reward amplifies the very positive-feedback runaway homeostasis opposes (Figure~\ref{fig:7u}). It is approached in two fidelities of the same dopaminergic reward-prediction error. The first is the non-invasive human correlate. Driven by the subjects' own \abbrev{EEG} reward positivity \cite{holroyd2002neural}, decoded from ds003474 \cite{cavanagh2019dissociations, chavarriaga2010learning, chavarriaga2014errare} at a single-trial balanced accuracy of $\sim$0.69, the deep stabilised agent rises off chance ($0.62$; Figure~\ref{fig:7w2}) while the unstabilised rule collapses, but does not clear the pre-registered separation bars. The result is real but decode-fidelity-limited, sharpening the question the capstone answers: is the ceiling set by the kind of biological signal, or by how cleanly it can be read?\\

The recorded transient separates reward from omission cleanly (Figure~\ref{fig:7v2}a). A cross-validated single-trial decoder of the post-outcome response reaches balanced accuracy $0.89$ (\abbrevdef{AUC} $0.92$) across ten sessions (Figure~\ref{fig:7v2}b), markedly cleaner than the cortical reward positivity above ($\sim$0.69), consistent with the dopamine transient being the more direct measurement.\\

As a single-layer precursor the device-trace bandit learns under the dopamine reward ($0.84$, $[0.82, 0.86]$ over 20 seeds), disjoint from its shuffled-reward control ($0.49$, $[0.47, 0.52]$; Figure~\ref{fig:7w}a). The deep test then holds (Figure~\ref{fig:7w}b,c): under the dopamine reward the stabilised deep agent learns ($0.92$, $[0.88, 0.96]$, fifteen of twenty seeds solving) while the unstabilised rule does not ($0.65$, $[0.57, 0.73]$), the two disjoint and both shuffled controls at chance. The agent clears every pre-registered separation bar the lower-fidelity \abbrev{EEG} version (Figure~\ref{fig:7w2}) could not, confirming the limit was decode \emph{fidelity}, not the kind of signal. Cross-species and offline, this is replay of a recorded mouse-dopamine reward rather than the agent's own brain. It establishes biorealism and robustness, not a new device capability.\\

\subsection[A Scaling Relation for Temporal Credit Assignment]{A Scaling Relation for Temporal Credit Assignment}
\label{sec:7p3p3}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/retention_delay_scaling_law.png}}
    \caption[The retention--delay scaling relation $D_{\max}\approx s_D\,\tau$.]{The maximum learnable action--reward delay $D_{\max}$ against the eligibility-trace retention $\tau_{leak}$, from a dense sequential-T-maze sweep of thirteen retention values, all resolving a finite $D_{\max}$ on the extended delay grid (20 seeds; $D_{\max}$ interpolated at the $0.75$ criterion). A line through the origin over all points gives $s_D\approx15$ ($R^2=0.99$). The local slope $D_{\max}/\tau_{leak}$ is not constant: it rises from $\approx7$ to a plateau of $\approx16$ as the cascade rise time becomes a negligible fraction of $\tau_{leak}$, so the relation is strictly linear only in the trace-dominated plateau ($\tau_{leak}\ge20$\,s). The predicted inverted-U saturation lies beyond the resolved range.}
    \label{fig:7l}
\end{figure}

\noindent A single quantitative regularity runs through every learning result in this chapter: the maximum learnable action--reward delay grows with the trace retention time. That retention is a physical, fabrication-set property of the oxide rather than a tuned hyperparameter. The dependence is therefore a physically grounded scaling relation for temporal credit assignment, tying a material time constant directly to a learnable behavioural timescale, and is the chapter's most consequential prediction.\\

\noindent A dense sweep of the sequential T-maze over thirteen retention values (Figure~\ref{fig:7l}), all resolving a finite $D_{\max}$ on an extended delay grid (from $D_{\max}=7$\,s at $\tau_{leak}=1$\,s to $638$\,s at $\tau_{leak}=40$\,s), gives its form as a proportionality,
\begin{align}
    D_{\max} \approx s_D\,\tau, \label{eq:7.15}
\end{align}
where $\tau$ is the eligibility-trace lifetime ($\tau_{leak}$ in the device, the neuromodulator-gated window in biology), so the relation is stated generally and instantiated per substrate. A line through the origin over all thirteen points gives $s_D\approx15$ ($R^2=0.99$).\\

\noindent The local slope $D_{\max}/\tau_{leak}$ is not constant, however. It rises from $\approx7$ at the shortest retentions to a plateau of $\approx16$ for $\tau_{leak}\gtrsim20$\,s, because the cascade rise time consumes a shrinking fraction of the window as $\tau_{leak}$ grows. The relation is therefore strictly linear, with constant slope, only in the trace-dominated plateau where the decay tail alone sets the reach. Mechanistically this is unsurprising: a coincidence whose trace has decayed below the plasticity threshold by reward time can no longer be credited, so the longest bridgeable delay is set by the trace lifetime. Izhikevich notes qualitatively that the trace ``decay rate controls the sensitivity of plasticity to delayed reward'' \cite{izhikevich2007solving}, and three-factor theory rests on the trace bounding the credit window \cite{fremaux2016neuromodulated, gerstner2018eligibility}. The explicit linear form of Equation~\ref{eq:7.15}, obtained by parametrically varying $\tau_{leak}$, is to the author's knowledge not previously stated or measured.\\

\noindent The linear form is the expected consequence of threshold-crossing on a monotonically decaying trace and is not the contribution. The empirical content is the explicit proportionality, with a slope read off from a measured material time constant, and its use as a \emph{fabrication design rule}: read in reverse, $\tau_{leak} \gtrsim D/s_D$ specifies the device retention a target action--reward delay demands. Two caveats bound it. First, $s_D$ is a local slope rather than a universal constant: it is task-dependent, and within this task settles to $\approx16$ only above the cascade rise time. Second, the relation is expected to be bounded rather than unlimited, since behavioural conditioning shows the cue--reward interval function is an inverted-U with a sharp falloff. The present sweep, trace-decay-limited by construction, does not reach that regime, remaining linear out to $\tau_{leak}=40$\,s ($D_{\max}\approx638$\,s), so saturation is expected but not observed here. The claim is therefore an approximately linear relation in the trace-dominated regime, not a saturating law over the full range.\\

\noindent The scaling sweep shows that retention \emph{tunes} the learnable delay, but on its own it cannot show retention to be an \emph{asset}: there $\tau_{leak}$ both sets the trace decay and bounds the bridgeable delay, so the proportionality is partly built in by construction. A separate control settles this. Holding the task, network, and budget fixed, a long-retention trace is contrasted with a short one on a distal-cue problem in which a single cue must be bridged to a delayed reward through the deep, two-layer all-local network of Figure~\ref{fig:7n}. Across cue--reward gaps from $1$ to $25$\,s the long-retention trace ($\tau_{leak} = 20$\,s) holds between $0.81$ and $0.94$, while a short-retention trace ($\tau_{leak} = 1.5$\,s) and a no-trace control stay at chance, its confidence interval disjoint from both at every gap (at the $10$\,s gap, $0.90$ $[0.84, 0.96]$ against $0.50$ and $0.47$; 20 seeds, Figure~\ref{fig:7dc}a). The advantage persists under the same measured device-fault prior used at array scale above (Figure~\ref{fig:7as}): at a $30\%$ stuck-off fault rate the long trace stays disjoint from the short one for every gap beyond a few seconds (at $10$\,s, $0.86$ $[0.79, 0.93]$ against $0.53$ $[0.48, 0.59]$; Figure~\ref{fig:7dc}b), the two tied only at the shortest gap where there is nothing to bridge. Using the device's own short-retention trace as the control, rather than a no-trace baseline, isolates retention as the operative variable: the effect is behavioural in scale, appearing at gaps of a few seconds and beyond, exactly where a short trace fails.\\

\noindent One further question is whether the relation, derived for the single-rate trace, survives the measured dispersion. The scaling sweep was repeated at the dispersed exponent. Across $\beta_{leak} = 1$ (single-rate), $0.85$ (the field-free operating value), and $0.54$ (the held-bias stress case of Section~\ref{sec:7p1p1}), the slope $s_D$ is unchanged on the coarse validation grid, and the multi-timescale homeostatic coupling of Section~\ref{sec:8p1p1} holds at ceiling at every exponent (Figure~\ref{fig:7bs}). The dispersion rescales the trace shape but not the retention--delay relation or what the network learns from it, confirming the single-rate reduction used throughout is the correct operating-regime form rather than a convenience.\\

\noindent The relation is a testable prediction, but its decisive tests lie outside the present in-simulation sweep, where $\tau_{leak}$ both sets the trace decay and bounds the bridgeable delay so the proportionality is partly built in. The tests fall into two arms. In the device, $\tau_{leak}=R_{leak}C$ is set by trap-release kinetics and engineerable at fabrication (Chapter~5), so changing the \emph{physical} retention should rescale $D_{\max}$ proportionally. Fabricating devices of differing $R_{leak}C$ and measuring their $D_{\max}$ is the decisive on-hardware test. It is not performed here, where $\tau_{leak}$ is a model parameter, and would ground the fabrication-tunability claim beyond the field-acceleration law Chapter~5 already measures (a held-bias $\sim$1.3\,s discharge following $\tau_{leak}(V)\propto e^{-0.89\sqrt{V}}$, extrapolating to a field-free value of a few seconds).\\

\noindent In biology, $\tau$ is the neuromodulator-gated synaptic eligibility window, itself tunable: dopamine acts on dendritic spines within $\sim$0.3--2\,s \cite{yagishita2014timewindow}, cortical and striatal eligibility traces persist for seconds \cite{he2015eligibility, fisher2017reward, shindou2019silent}, and hippocampal traces extend to tens of seconds \cite{brzosko2015retroactive}. Equation~\ref{eq:7.15} therefore predicts that manipulating the molecular eligibility lifetime, for instance by a Yagishita-style optogenetic shift of the dopamine timing window, should rescale the maximum bridgeable cue--reward interval proportionally within the trace-dominated regime. This arm is a prediction the model makes, not a result obtained here, and the correspondence remains dynamical (a matched relaxation timescale) rather than chemical, as flagged in Section~\ref{sec:7p1p1}.\\

\noindent The electrode substitution of Section~\ref{sec:5p-ito} is a complementary experiment that should not be mistaken for this $R_{leak}C$ test. Gold and \abbrev{ITO} stacks share the same intrinsic trap discharge $\tau_{leak}$. The electrode changes only its visibility, gold superimposing the slow proton branch that masks it and \abbrev{ITO} stripping that branch away to expose the bare relaxation (the few-second discharge measured in Section~\ref{sec:5p-ito}). That exposure is what makes $\tau_{leak}$ measurable, grounding the eligibility window in a measured constant rather than a hand-set one. It does not itself shift the retention, however, and is a measurement enabler, not the tunability knob. The decisive test of Equation~\ref{eq:7.15} remains a series of devices engineered to differing $\tau_{leak}$ through trap energetics.\\

\noindent The slow space-charge branch ($\tau_d$, tens to hundreds of seconds) is not a longer-delay alternative. It is the ionic screening \abbrev{ITO} removes, monotonic rather than coincidence-peaked, so driving credit from it would lose temporal selectivity by crediting in-window distractors and would leave the biological window. The eligibility is the electronic trap discharge alone.\\

\noindent The claim is bounded by exactly the limitation Chapter~5 records: the \abbrev{ITO} extension transfers the dispersion exponent but not yet a pinned rate law, since the measured $\tau_{leak}$ scatters from device to device and several devices ran into the measurement compliance limit. The prediction is thus qualitative in the slope and awaits a more uniform, lower-current \abbrev{ITO} set to quantify $s_D$ across electrodes. At this strength, electrode-engineered control of the eligibility timescale is a hypothesis the model and Chapter~5 jointly motivate, not a demonstrated result.\\

\noindent The idea that a device's own relaxation can serve as an eligibility trace is not new, and the contribution is best stated against its precedents. The earliest is PCM-trace, which reduces the resistivity-drift power law of a two-terminal phase-change cell to the eligibility-trace decay equation \cite{demiraug2021pcm}. The closest concurrent work is Wang \emph{et al.}, who use the ferroelectric polarisation relaxation of a three-terminal $\alpha$-In$_2$Se$_3$ synaptic transistor as an eligibility trace for on-device spiking reinforcement learning \cite{wang2026ferroelectric}. An analogue-memristor actor--critic network performs reward-based learning on the same two-terminal oxide device class as this chapter, but writes its weights with explicit voltage pulses and leaves eligibility traces to future work \cite{portner2025actorcritic}. A two-terminal second-order oxide memristor drives continual reinforcement learning from an intrinsic oxygen gradient, framed as a physically modulated learning rate rather than a relaxation eligibility trace \cite{ming2026oxygen}.\\

\noindent The construction here is distinct on three axes. The mechanism is the \emph{intrinsic charge-trap discharge} of a filamentary silicon oxide, identified by reduction of a fitted current-transient model, not structural drift, a gate-modulated ferroelectric field, or an externally applied write. The retention obeys the explicit, testable retention--delay relation of Equation~\ref{eq:7.15}, $D_{\max}\approx s_D\,\tau_{leak}$, obtained by parametrically tuning the trace lifetime, and matches the measured biological eligibility window of Section~\ref{sec:7p1p1} quantitatively. And electrode choice decouples the device's dual-phase transient. The gold contact masks the fast trap discharge behind the slow proton decay, and indium--tin-oxide exposes it (Section~\ref{sec:5p-ito}), making the credit window directly measurable rather than inferred. None of these is reported for the precedent devices.

\section[Summary]{Summary}

\noindent This chapter reinterpreted the silicon-oxide current transient, modelled in Chapter~5 as a non-ideality, as the eligibility trace of reward-modulated learning. The dispersive trap-cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:5p3} reduces to the canonical trace equation, with the trap-discharge retention $\tau_{leak}=R_{leak}C$ as its time constant. As the eligibility term of a signed three-factor rule, the trace assigned distal-reward credit at the trace level, in a spiking network, and in a closed contextual bandit. It then reached ceiling on a sequential T-maze, alongside the R-STDP and e-prop baselines. The difference is that it supplies its retention as a fabrication-set property, not a hyperparameter. Pushed to depth, two layers of device synapses learned a non-linearly-separable task fully locally, with no weight transport. This held only once a local homeostatic stabiliser removed a policy-collapse instability. That stabiliser is the same mechanism the device required for static inference in Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}. One device family therefore supplies both of biology's slow local primitives.\\

\noindent The rule was grounded in dopaminergic learning in two ways: \emph{behaviourally}, the device reproduced the human positive- versus negative-feedback asymmetry on the Frank task, and \emph{mechanistically}, the capstone learned under a reward decoded from a directly measured dopamine transient -- nucleus-accumbens fibre photometry recorded in mice (Jeong et al., \abbrev{DANDI} 000351), reused from that public dataset and not measured in this work. Across every task the longest learnable delay scaled with retention as $D_{\max}\approx s_D\,\tau_{leak}$. This is a testable design rule, and it holds at the measured dispersion as well as the single-rate limit. The all-local rule also degraded gradually to array scale under the measured device-fault prior. The device's slow dynamics are a drawback for static inference. They are, however, matched to the timescale of reward learning. This motivates an on-chip implementation with the reward delivered in hardware. The edges of the primitive and a predicted device-internal capture mechanism, on which these results do not depend, are taken up as exploratory work in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}.
