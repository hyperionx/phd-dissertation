\chapter{Memristive Reinforcement Learning}\label{ch:learning}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} trained static weights robust to the device's \emph{steady-state} non-idealities, setting the slow current transient aside. This chapter takes the complementary route: it uses the transient of Chapters~\ref{ch:transients} and~5 as the computational substrate itself. The dispersive trap-cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:5p3} supplies a device-local analogue of an \emph{eligibility state}, the slowly decaying synaptic memory that reward-modulated learning needs to bridge an action--reward delay. Trace-level, leaky integrate-and-fire (\abbrev{LIF}) and closed contextual-bandit experiments establish the device-to-learning construction; a four-decision task then tests trajectory-level credit. Learning-rate-fair controls define its operating envelope, while the central shape result survives them: the measured rise--decay form supplies an interval-selective temporal prior under the stated circuit abstraction.\\

\noindent A deep all-local two-layer agent then tests how temporal and spatial credit compose, reaching reliable learning once the same-class local homeostatic stabiliser is added. These archived deep results use the linear cascade representation and are retained as synthetic representation sensitivities. The biological spine is supplied by three complementary public datasets: Coddington provides a positive closed-loop test of action-contingent dopamine policy learning, Jeong independently recovers the direction of dopamine-inhibition effects, and DANDI 001340 connects intact continuous dLight waveforms to the recorded-action update. The physical replay improves held-out choice likelihood relative to within-session shuffled waveforms; the similarity of matched kernels defines the next shape-identification experiment rather than weakening this biological connection. Retention--delay scaling is reported as a task-specific design relation whose physical test is fixed prospectively.\\

\noindent The contributions of this chapter are entirely the author's own. The identification of the device transient as an eligibility trace, the three-factor learning rule built on it, and every experiment, simulation, and analysis reported here were conceived and carried out by the author, building on the device model of Chapter~\ref{ch:model} and the homeostatic mechanism of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}.

\section[The Transient as an Eligibility Trace]{The Transient as an Eligibility Trace}
\label{sec:7p1}

\noindent Reinforcement learning in any spiking substrate faces the \emph{distal-reward problem} of Section~\ref{subsec:learning-paradigms} \cite{izhikevich2007solving}: a reward arrives well after the spike coincidences that earned it. The established biological resolution is the three-factor, or neo-Hebbian, eligibility-trace rule \cite{fremaux2016neuromodulated, gerstner2018eligibility}. In that rule a fast pre--post coincidence sets a slowly decaying \emph{eligibility trace} that a delayed neuromodulator later gates into a weight change. Where the slow trace comes from is usually left open, the trace posited as an abstract synaptic variable. This chapter tests whether the measured device transient can supply a physically local analogue of that state. Its decay time constrains a task-specific action--reward window in simulation; it does not by itself establish the biological trace or a universal learnable horizon \cite{izhikevich2007solving, florian2007reinforcement}.

\subsection[Repurposing the Trap-Cascade Model]{Repurposing the Trap-Cascade Model}
\label{sec:7p1p1}

\noindent The dispersive trap-cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:5p3} realises this candidate trace computationally. The compressed rise is consistent with a short effective cascade, represented primarily by $k=3$ with $k=4$ as a sensitivity; the data do not uniquely identify microscopic stage count. Chapter~5 fits the transient under constant bias. Here the same equation family is driven by a pre--post coincidence and read as a normalised gating variable. The nonlinear normalised-headroom equation is the equation-matching physical primary, while the earlier linear Erlang cascade is retained explicitly as the empirically better Au representation and computational sensitivity.\\

\noindent Concretely, the same system of first-order ordinary differential equations (\abbrev{ODE}s) is retained, but a coincidence signal $d_{ij}(t)$ at synapse $ij$ replaces the applied bias as the cascade drive, and the normalised final stage is read out as the eligibility trace,
\begin{align}
    \frac{dv^{(m)}_{ij}}{dt} &= \alpha\, p^{(m)}_{ij}\left( V_{max} - \left| v^{(m)}_{ij} \right| \right) - \frac{v^{(m)}_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}}, \label{eq:7.1} \\
    \frac{du_{ij}}{dt} &= \alpha\, d_{ij}\left( V_{max} - \left| u_{ij} \right| \right) - \frac{u_{ij}}{\tau_d}, \label{eq:7.2} \\
    e_{ij}(t) &= \frac{v^{(k)}_{ij}(t)}{V_{max}}, \label{eq:7.3}
\end{align}
with $p^{(1)}_{ij} = d_{ij}$ and $p^{(m)}_{ij} = v^{(m-1)}_{ij}/V_{max}$ for $m>1$ (the sequential cascade of Section~\ref{sec:5p3}, the upstream stage entering as a dimensionless occupancy fraction), and $\alpha = k/\tau_r$ the per-stage rate set by the fitted rise time. Equation~\ref{eq:7.2} is the slow space-charge state $u_{ij}$ that Chapter~5 separates from the trap chain, driven by the same coincidence $d_{ij}$ but relaxing at the proton-decay constant $\tau_d \gg \tau_{leak}$. It screens the interfacial field but carries no appreciable current. The eligibility is read from the final cascade stage alone (Equation~\ref{eq:7.3}), so $u_{ij}$ does not enter it. The $\left| \cdot \right|$ headroom term admits signed (depressing) excursions bounded to $\left| v \right|, \left| u \right| \le V_{max}$, a generalisation of Equations~\ref{eq:5.6}--\ref{eq:5.7} required because an eligibility trace must decrease as well as increase. The rise is the same sigmoidal trap-filling of Chapter~5 and the relaxation the same $R_{leak}$ leakage. Only the interpretation changes. The retention $\tau_{leak}$, a short-term-memory time in Chapter~5, becomes the \emph{credit-assignment window}: the longest coincidence-to-reward delay over which the synapse remains eligible.\\

\noindent The correspondence is exact at the level of the reduced equation, but it is not a demonstration of biological chemistry or of the surrounding circuit. Take a single state ($k=1$, so $p^{(1)}_{ij} = d_{ij}$) and the small-signal regime $|v| \ll V_{max}$, where the headroom factor $V_{max} - |v| \to V_{max}$ is constant. Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} becomes linear,
\begin{align}
    \dot{e}_{ij} \;\approx\; \underbrace{\alpha}_{\text{gain}}\, d_{ij}(t) \;-\; \frac{e_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}}, \label{eq:7.4}
\end{align}
a leaky integrator driven by the pre--post coincidence $d_{ij}$ and decaying with time constant $\tau_{leak}$. This is, term by term, the canonical eligibility-trace equation of reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity \cite{izhikevich2007solving, florian2007reinforcement}: the coincidence drive plays the role of the \abbrev{STDP} kernel and the trap-discharge time $\tau_{leak}$ that of the trace time constant. The device model can therefore implement the same state equation locally, provided a circuit supplies coincidence drive, sensing and reward-gated weight update. That mathematical identity does not establish that the device and a biological synapse share a microscopic mechanism.\\

\noindent Under this circuit abstraction, the candidate eligibility state is carried by the device's own relaxation rather than by a separately stored digital trajectory. Figure~\ref{fig:7s} traces the proposed mapping at the electron level, from coincidence-driven trap capture, through the trapped-electron population used as eligibility, to the reward-gated update that samples whatever survives at $t_R$.\\

\noindent This makes the construction biorealistic in learning-rule structure, not biologically identified in mechanism. It is functionally related to \emph{synaptic tagging and capture} \cite{frey1997synaptic, redondo2011making}: a coincidence sets a transient \emph{tag} that decays on its own, and a delayed neuromodulator captures it into a lasting change only while the tag survives \cite{frey1997synaptic, moncada2007induction}. The shared requirement is a physical transient state with its own decay time; the chemical substrate is different. Cortical synapses carry distinct potentiation and depression traces \cite{he2015eligibility}, and dopamine reinforces a recently stimulated spine only within roughly $0.3$--$2$\,s \cite{yagishita2014timewindow, gerstner2018eligibility}.\\

\noindent The correspondence is to the logic of tagging and capture, a decaying tag whose lifetime sets a capture window, and is dynamical rather than chemical. Trap emission through $R_{leak}$ is not the synapse's calcium- and protein-based machinery. The claim is therefore the weaker one that both are activity-set relaxation processes on the same behavioural timescale, not the minutes-to-hours chemistry of classical tagging and capture. Figure~\ref{fig:7v} sets the two sequences side by side.\\

\noindent Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} is physically motivated rather than microscopically identified term by term. Conduction in this silicon-rich oxide is filamentary and compatible with inelastic trap-assisted tunnelling between defect sites \cite{buckwell2015conductance,jimenez2001physical}. A short cascade is consistent with the compressed rise, but the historical averaged-trace estimate $\beta_{fill}=1.95\pm0.30$ and the grouped full-fit value 2.64 differ enough to rule out a precise stage-count claim. The frozen $k=3$ primary and $k=4$ sensitivity are therefore representation choices. Their rise-rate law is fitted; coincidence coupling, signed headroom and reward capture are repository adaptations that require circuit validation.\\

\noindent The leak term $-v/\tau_{leak}$ is the discharge of those same traps once the coincidence drive stops. Because the traps span a distribution of release energies rather than a single rate, the discharge is \emph{dispersive}, a stretched exponential ($\beta_{leak} < 1$): the Scher--Montroll signature of relaxation over a spread of trap depths \cite{scher1975anomalous}, and the mirror image of the compressed sequential fill.\\

\noindent The space-charge branch of Equation~\ref{eq:7.2}, which Chapter~5 separates from this discharge, is associated with a mobile positive species that screens the interfacial field. Only the faster residual trap-discharge branch is used for eligibility. Its measured timescale constrains the model, but not every learning coefficient is a measured material property: coincidence gain, reward coupling, selected synthetic retentions and update scale remain modelling or circuit choices.\\

\noindent This dispersive picture also yields the eligibility trace from first principles, and shows the canonical exponential trace to be a limiting case of it. Take the trace to be the population of electrons still held in the conducting traps a time $t$ after a coincidence empties charge into them at $t=0$. A single trap of release-energy $E$ emits to the conduction band at a thermally-activated first-order rate
\begin{align}
    r(E) = \nu_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{E}{k_B T}\right), \label{eq:7.5}
\end{align}
with attempt frequency $\nu_0$, so its occupancy decays as $e^{-r(E)t}$. The amorphous oxide does not present one depth but a distribution $g(E)$ of trap energies, the very dispersion that gives the stretched discharge, so the measurable eligibility is the ensemble average over that distribution,
\begin{align}
    e(t) = \int_0^{\infty} g(E)\, \exp\!\big(-r(E)\,t\big)\, dE. \label{eq:7.6}
\end{align}
For the exponential band tail $g(E) \propto \exp(-E/E_0)$ characteristic of disordered silicon-rich oxides, with disorder width $E_0$, this multiple-trapping integral is the Scher--Montroll problem \cite{scher1975anomalous, kakalios1987stretched}: its long-time form is the stretched (dispersive) exponential
\begin{align}
    e(t) \approx \exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{t}{\tau_{leak}}\right)^{\beta_{leak}}\right], \qquad \beta_{leak} = \frac{k_B T}{E_0} < 1, \label{eq:7.7}
\end{align}
whose exponent $\beta_{leak}$ is set by the disorder width $E_0$ relative to thermal energy and whose scale $\tau_{leak}$ is the trap-discharge retention. Equation~\ref{eq:7.7} describes the held-bias \abbrev{ITO} records of Chapter~5 (primary-QC $\beta_{leak}=0.470\pm0.090$). In the narrow-distribution limit it collapses to the canonical mono-exponential trace. The two supplementary 1\,mV reads give $\beta_{leak}=0.836$ and 0.814, suggesting milder near-zero dispersion but not establishing a field-free population. The single-rate path is consequently an explicit computational reduction whose sensitivity is tested, not a uniquely validated operating-regime law.\\

\noindent Two features make the correspondence useful. First, the measured discharge constrains a seconds-scale family rather than leaving retention wholly arbitrary. Second, the $k>1$ representations depart from an instantaneous-rise trace through a sigmoidal latency. A digital simulation must store and update these states explicitly; an in-material implementation could instead retain them physically. That state-locality benefit is conditional on a circuit that supplies coincidence, sensing and reward gating, whose costs are not measured here.\\

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7a} shows the resulting trace for three retention settings: a sigmoidal rise of seconds followed by a relaxation whose duration is set by $\tau_{leak}$. Two elements are imposed by the surrounding circuit rather than intrinsic to the device. The coincidence drive $d_{ij}(t)$ and the reward gating below are encodings placed around a two-terminal device that does not itself compute coincidence or sense reward; the device supplies the \emph{trace dynamics}, the network the \emph{three-factor structure}.\\

\noindent The gold-device fits span $\tau_r\approx1.9$--$14.5$\,s and $\tau_d\approx36$--$537$\,s. The primary \abbrev{ITO} held-bias median is 1.390\,s, the field-law zero-bias extrapolation is 2.55\,s $[1.73,3.85]$, and the two supplementary 1\,mV reads are 4.31 and 9.20\,s. These definitions must not be pooled as one measured band. They overlap the seconds-scale biological eligibility literature \cite{yagishita2014timewindow, he2015eligibility}, but dynamical overlap is not chemical equivalence.\\

\noindent The learning values $\tau_{leak}=1$, 5, 10 and 20\,s are model settings, not separately programmed devices. The 1-s point is near the held-bias distribution, 5--10\,s overlap the two preliminary near-zero reads, and 20\,s is a synthetic target. The primary sequential comparison below declares $\tau_{leak}=10$\,s explicitly as synthetic.\\

\subsection[Kernel Theory and Representation Identifiability]{Kernel Theory and Representation Identifiability}
\label{sec:7p1p3}

\noindent The linear cascade admits an exact small-signal result that separates rise from leakage. For an impulse-like deposit its final state is proportional to
\begin{align}
    e(t) &\propto t^{k-1}\exp(-\lambda t), &
    \lambda &= \frac{k}{\tau_r}+\frac{1}{\tau_{leak}}, &
    t^\ast &= \frac{k-1}{\lambda}. \label{eq:7.8a}
\end{align}
Writing $\rho=(k/\tau_r)\tau_{leak}$ gives a leakage-limited peak $t^\ast\approx(k-1)\tau_{leak}$ for $\rho\ll1$ and a fill-limited peak $t^\ast\approx(k-1)\tau_r/k$ for $\rho\gg1$. A finite rectangular coincidence is the difference of two Erlang step responses; its peak is obtained from the unique deterministic derivative root. The impulse expression is therefore the short-pulse limit rather than the equation used for the plotted finite deposit.\\

\noindent The nonlinear headroom equation is not globally a Gamma kernel. It approaches the downstream Gamma envelope only at low occupancy after drive-off; finite deposits introduce saturation and shift the preferred lag. Numerical integration over voltage, retention, depth, deposit duration and $V_{max}$ verifies the analytic linear result and quantifies the nonlinear departure (Figure~\ref{fig:7kernel}a,b). The voltage axis is a bias-conditioned model prediction, not evidence that a measured circuit can program its preferred interval.\\

\noindent Grouped leave-one-bias-out prediction provides the primary representation test on the six in-domain Au biases, with 1.7 and 1.8\,V reserved as untouched extrapolation diagnostics. Linear Erlang $k=3$ gives the lowest NRMSE, 0.0548, but KWW and every linear depth $k=2$--5 remain within 5\% of that score in all 2,000 repeat-stratified held-out-prediction rescores. The nonlinear candidates are worse (NRMSE 0.1315--0.1318) and receive zero within-tolerance support. Separately, 2,000 full KWW refits give $\beta_{fill}=2.60$ [2.44, 2.78], resolving a compressed rise but not an integer stage count. A Gaussian AICc/BIC working likelihood selects linear $k=2$ and is kept secondary because the fitted residuals are temporally correlated and the primary fits use robust loss.\\

\noindent This distinction reconciles physical motivation with empirical model selection. The nonlinear equation matches the proposed device-level headroom mechanism and remains the primary learning abstraction; the linear cascade and KWW form describe the measured Au transient better. None of those representations identifies a microscopic sequence of traps, and their fitted scores are not used to tune a downstream learning or dopamine-replay parameter.\\

\subsection[The Three-Factor Learning Rule]{The Three-Factor Learning Rule}
\label{sec:7p1p2}

\noindent The device trace serves as the eligibility term $e_{ij}(t)$ of a three-factor rule. A pre--post coincidence drives the device state; a delayed global reward then gates the surviving trace into a weight change,
\begin{align}
    \Delta w_{ij} = \eta \left( R - b \right) e_{ij}(t_R), \label{eq:7.8}
\end{align}
where $t_R$ is the reward time, $R$ the reward on the trial, $b$ a slowly adapting reward baseline (an advantage estimate), and $\eta$ the learning rate. The reward enters as a global scalar setting both the sign and magnitude of plasticity. On under-reward ($R<b$) the active synapses are depressed \cite{fremaux2016neuromodulated, florian2007reinforcement}.\\

\noindent A selective trace alone does not yield selective weights: reward-uncorrelated synapses that happen to be active also accumulate eligibility, and without a counterbalancing term every weight drifts together. Following the canonical resolution of the distal-reward problem \cite{izhikevich2007solving}, the coincidence kernel driving the device state is made \emph{leak-dominant} (signed): a causal pre-then-post coincidence contributes positively, while presynaptic activity that does not drive the postsynaptic neuron contributes a smaller negative term,
\begin{align}
    d_{ij}(t) = x^{pre}_i(t) \left[ \mathbf{1}\!\left( x^{post}_j(t) \right) - \lambda_d \left( 1 - \mathbf{1}\!\left( x^{post}_j(t) \right) \right) \right], \label{eq:7.9}
\end{align}
where $x^{pre}_i$ and $x^{post}_j$ are the pre- and postsynaptic spike trains, $\mathbf{1}(\cdot)$ indicates a postsynaptic spike in the time step, and $\lambda_d \in (0,1)$ sets the acausal-depression weight. A reward-correlated cue produces mostly causal coincidences and a net-positive trace. An uncorrelated distractor produces mostly acausal activity and a net-negative trace that decays rather than drifting up. This signed eligibility, rather than weight normalisation, makes the rewarded synapse win.\\

\noindent In the spiking network the postsynaptic neuron follows the \abbrev{LIF} unit of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} (Equations~\ref{eq:6.1}--\ref{eq:6.2}), here with an explicit leakage and an added membrane-noise term,
\begin{align}
    V^{m}_j(t + \Delta t) = V^{m}_j(t) + \sum_i w_{ij}\, x^{pre}_i(t) - \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_m} V^{m}_j(t) + \sigma\, \xi_j(t), \label{eq:7.10}
\end{align}
emitting a spike and resetting when $V^{m}_j \ge V_{th}$. Here $\xi_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ is per-neuron noise of scale $\sigma$ providing exploration, and $\tau_m$ is the membrane time constant. The architecture and per-synapse mechanism are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:7b}.

\section[Closed-Loop Reinforcement Learning]{Closed-Loop Reinforcement Learning}
\label{sec:7p2}

\noindent The rule is evaluated first in a reduced trace-level model isolating the credit-assignment function, then in a full \abbrev{LIF} spiking network in which the device state is integrated online from real spike coincidences, and finally in a closed reinforcement-learning loop. All three use the canonical distal-reward task. A cue synapse undergoes a coincidence, a reward is delivered after an action--reward delay $D$ with interleaved unrewarded catch trials, and reward-uncorrelated distractor synapses are active throughout. A correct learner raises the cue weight and leaves the distractors near baseline despite the delay. Success is measured as in the established literature \cite{izhikevich2007solving, legenstein2008learning}: at the trace level by the cue-to-distractor weight ratio, and in the spiking network by saturation of the cue synapse toward its conductance bound while distractors stay at baseline.

\subsection[Retention as a Credit-Assignment Window]{Retention as a Credit-Assignment Window}
\label{sec:7p2p1}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7c} reports the trace-level result. The no-trace control never learns (ratio at unity for all delays), confirming the trace is necessary. The device trace assigns credit, and the maximum learnable delay scales monotonically with retention: $\tau_{leak} = 1$, $5$, and $20$\,s learn delays up to roughly $5$, $40$, and $100$\,s respectively. The long-retention trace is, as expected, weaker at the shortest delays, because its sigmoidal rise has not yet peaked when a near-immediate reward arrives. Retention therefore trades short-delay against long-delay performance, a tunable device property rather than a fixed kernel. This short-delay weakness is the visible edge of a more general property, examined next: the rise makes the trace \emph{peaked} rather than monotone, so it does not merely bridge a delay but selects a preferred one.\\

\noindent This peakedness is a qualitative difference from the canonical trace, with a behavioural consequence so far left implicit. The exponential trace of reward-modulated \abbrev{STDP} is monotone, largest at lag zero and decaying thereafter. It is \emph{recency}-weighted, crediting whatever coincided just before the reward. The device trace, built from the trap-cascade rise of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1}, instead peaks at a non-zero lag $t^\ast$ and falls away on both sides (Figure~\ref{fig:7o}a). It is a band-pass in time: it credits a coincidence at its \emph{preferred interval} and suppresses one at lag zero.\\

\noindent To test whether this changes what is learned, two perfectly reward-correlated synapses were placed in competition. One fired at the model's preferred interval before reward and the other near reward. Across $\eta=0.01$, 0.1 and 0.5, the physical primary gave $S=w_{\rm pref}/w_{\rm late}$ of 3.98, 3.95 and 3.88, while its independently decay-matched exponential gave 0.62, 0.78 and 0.79. The linear sensitivity showed the same direction (device 3.76, 3.64, 3.58; exponential 0.58, 0.72, 0.73). Device $S>1$ and exponential $S<1$ therefore survive the learning-rate and representation sweeps. This amplitude-robust direction, not the older shared-rate comparison, is the primary kernel-shape evidence.\\

\noindent This does not make the frozen kernel universally useful. A second benchmark presents four candidate event lags in four predeclared timing families: physical-aligned, early-delay, recency and broad-delay. Candidate-score amplitude is normalised from pilot inputs without labels. The fixed physical and linear kernels and matched exponential are compared with a signed three-exponential basis having the same three dynamic states, whose shape is fitted on disjoint pilot blocks. At the physical kernel's synthetic preferred lag of 19.36\,s, physical log loss is 0.845 (top-one 0.672), close to the learned basis at 0.720 (0.688) and well ahead of the matched exponential at 2.491. Across heterogeneous families the learned basis is better overall (0.708 versus 2.041; paired physical-minus-learned 1.333 [0.700, 2.003]). The device is therefore a specialised temporal prior, not a generally preferable three-state filter.\\

\noindent The structural comparison states what this specialisation could buy. At 16-bit precision a digital exponential stores 16 dynamic-state bits per synapse, while the linear and learned three-state filters store 48. Under an in-material relaxation assumption the physical row stores no \emph{external} eligibility-state word; the learned basis additionally requires 6 shared decay/readout coefficients. This lower bound excludes cells, selectors, sensing, programming, reward routing, refresh and memory traffic. It therefore supports state locality, not a measured energy, area or throughput advantage. The representation/depth/voltage/retention model envelope places preferred lag between 0.55 and 31.95\,s; it is not a confidence interval.\\

\noindent A second amplitude-controlled battery removes the kernel-selected target interval. Four frozen shapes---nonlinear headroom, linear cascade, the linear cascade's Gamma limit and a post-peak decay-matched exponential---are compared under the same raw drive, unit peak, unit $L^1$ area and label-free pilot RMS scaling. Only one scalar temperature and intercept are fitted on pilot scenarios; learning-rate or readout fitting is kept separate from kernel matching. The four scale treatments agree to numerical tolerance, so update magnitude cannot create a timing advantage.\\

\noindent Across the combined four-event interval-attribution and two-cue temporal-order battery, the RMS-normalised nonlinear trace gives held-out loss 0.949, compared with 0.534 for the matched exponential. On interval attribution alone, their losses are 1.329 and 1.334 and their top-one accuracies 0.387 and 0.367; the paired loss difference is $-0.005$ [$-0.265$, $0.329$] and is unresolved across the predeclared absolute-delay grid. The scalar temporal-order task exposes the complementary limitation: the nonlinear trace reaches accuracy 0.478, whereas the monotone linear, Gamma and exponential readouts reach 1.000. Equal scalar values on the rising and falling limbs alias early and late events. Reading the internal stage vector resolves the construction in simulation, but that state is unavailable from the present two-terminal readout.\\

\noindent The tracked prediction artifact freezes three future tests before any new measurement: normalised pulse-off eligibility versus delay, preferred lag versus bias, and departure from the low-occupancy limit as headroom fills. \noindent To confirm the mechanism is not an artefact of the trace-level reduction, the device state is embedded as an online per-synapse trace in the spiking network of Figure~\ref{fig:7b}(a). Here $N=8$ Poisson inputs project through plastic synapses to one \abbrev{LIF} output neuron, each synapse integrating the leak-dominant coincidence kernel through the fitted device dynamics at every time step. Because the device relaxes on a seconds timescale, it integrates the coincidence \emph{rate} rather than resolving millisecond spike pairs. The action it marks is a sustained epoch of correlated firing, the regime where a slow trace is the appropriate primitive.\\

\noindent The preceding tasks fix the reward schedule externally. The four plastic synapses carry the device trace, updated at reward by the signed rule of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8}.\\

\subsection[Crossbar Realisation and Scaling to Depth]{Crossbar Realisation and Scaling to Depth}
\label{sec:7p3p1}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/closed_loop_crossbar.png}}
    \caption[Crossbar realisation of the closed-loop task.]{Crossbar realisation of the closed-loop task. (a) The contextual bandit mapped onto an $S \times A$ memristive array, with a single global third-factor line broadcasting $(R-b)$ to every crosspoint. (b) One crosspoint: a differential pair encoding the signed weight, the device transient supplying the local eligibility trace, and the reward-gated local update.}
    \label{fig:7f}
\end{figure}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:7f} is a proposed crossbar mapping, not a fabricated learning circuit. State inputs drive array rows and action neurons read bitline currents. Each signed weight $w_{ij}$ is encoded as a differential conductance pair $(G^{+}_{ij},G^{-}_{ij})$ on two bitlines (Equation~\ref{eq:6.28}), so the idealised current read by action neuron $j$ is
\begin{align}
    I_j = \sum_i \left( G^{+}_{ij} - G^{-}_{ij} \right) V^{in}_i, \qquad w_{ij} \propto G^{+}_{ij} - G^{-}_{ij}, \label{eq:7.11}
\end{align}
Kirchhoff summation along the column, the same ideal in-memory product used for inference in Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}. The proposed advantage is that material relaxation carries the eligibility state locally. Under this in-material assumption the structural accounting assigns zero externally stored eligibility-state bits to the physical row, versus 16 for a digital exponential and 48 for either three-state digital comparator at 16-bit precision. This excludes cells, selectors, sensing and routing; it is not a claim of zero memory or zero circuit cost.\\

\noindent The proposed weight update is locally addressable once the global reward is routed. Discretising Equation~\ref{eq:7.8} gives
\begin{align}
    \Delta G^{\pm}_{ij} = \pm\, \eta\, \big( R - b \big)\, e_{ij}(t_R), \label{eq:7.12}
\end{align}
applied as complementary potentiation/depression pulses. The rule needs neither a transposed weight matrix nor a per-synapse error signal \cite{bellec2020solution,zhou2022memprop}, but it does need coincidence generation, local eligibility sensing, reward-line gating and selective programming. A digital implementation of the model would update
\begin{align}
    v^{(m)}_{ij}(t{+}\Delta t) = v^{(m)}_{ij}(t) + \Delta t \left[ \alpha\, p^{(m)}_{ij} \big( V_{max} - |v^{(m)}_{ij}| \big) - \frac{v^{(m)}_{ij}}{\tau_{leak}} \right] \label{eq:7.13}
\end{align}
whereas the physical proposal replaces externally stored recurrence state with material relaxation. Whether the omitted peripheral costs outweigh that state saving requires circuit design and measurement.

\noindent Two implementation costs are specific to the two-terminal device. First, delivering the reward factor $(R-b)$ requires gating the programming pulse of Equation~\ref{eq:7.12} by a global signal, which a 1T1R cell or shared row/column driver supplies but a three-terminal synaptic transistor would provide natively through its gate, trading the two-terminal cell's density against cleaner third-factor routing. Second, the eligibility must persist between coincidence and reward, which the measured $\tau_{leak}$ provides over its seconds-scale window; longer delays would need a longer-retention variant or a refreshed capacitive hold. Both are circuit-design choices rather than obstacles to the rule, which is agnostic to how $e_{ij}$ and $(R-b)$ are physically realised.\\

\noindent A cheap update is not the same as a learnable policy. Generalising the bandit to an $S \times A$ array separates the two (Figure~\ref{fig:7g}a). The device trace is necessary and learns at every size tested, above chance while the no-trace control stays at chance. Within a fixed budget, however, it reaches the convergence criterion only up to about four actions, with trials-to-criterion growing steeply ($\sim$290, $\sim$460, and $\sim$820 trials for two, three, and four actions). This is the expected sample-efficiency cost of a larger action space under the undirected (membrane-noise) exploration used here, where a random action is rewarded with probability $1/A$.\\

\noindent Standard remedies remove the wall (Figure~\ref{fig:7g}b). Holding the device gate and learning rule fixed on the failing $8\times8$ case, quadrupling the budget restores convergence (from $0.31$ to $0.70$), and adding directed (annealed) exploration reaches $0.97$. Directed exploration alone at the original budget is insufficient. Substituting an abstract exponential trace for the device at the original budget also clears the criterion ($0.75$) where the device does not, confirming the wall is one of samples rather than of the rule. This also exposes a genuine device cost: the slow sigmoidal rise has not peaked when a near-immediate reward arrives (the low-delay dip of Figures~\ref{fig:7c}--\ref{fig:7d}), a real but surmountable sample-efficiency penalty. Reaching large action spaces thus calls for the usual reinforcement-learning machinery, directed exploration, reward shaping, hierarchy, or function approximation across states, layered on this primitive rather than replacing it.\\

The slow transient is thus confined to the decision layer where the action--reward loop lives, rather than threaded through a network whose millisecond inference its seconds-scale dynamics could not match. The perception stage therefore enables the device primitive on high-dimensional input but does not replace it.\\

\noindent The hybrid agent confines the device trace to a shallow decision layer behind a separately-trained front-end. A sharper question is whether the trace can instead train a network of more than one plastic layer entirely locally, carrying credit through depth as well as time. The experiment establishes that the deep agent learns but unreliably, then diagnoses the cause and removes it with a local mechanism already developed in this thesis, ending in a reliable deep all-local learner within bounded scope.\\

\noindent The task is an exclusive-or (XOR) contextual bandit: two binary cue features, the rewarded action their XOR, encoded so the four cue patterns carry equal total input drive. XOR is not linearly separable \cite{rumelhart1986learning}. The archived network uses two layers of signed synapses and the linear-Erlang eligibility representation on both weight matrices. What varies between conditions is the spatial-credit pathway. Because the nonlinear physical default changed after this archive was produced, these values remain sensitivity evidence only.\\

\noindent The global-scalar rule broadcasts the same advantage $(R-b)$ of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8} to every synapse in both layers, as in the single-layer tasks. Direct feedback alignment (\abbrevdef{DFA}) instead gives the hidden layer a \emph{per-neuron} learning signal, projecting the output error through a fixed random feedback matrix \cite{lillicrap2016random}. Because that matrix is fixed and independent of the forward weights, the update needs no weight transport and stays as local and crossbar-compatible as the global rule. Three controls bound the result: a single trained layer (shallow), a fixed-random hidden layer with only the output trained (an extreme-learning-machine, or \abbrevdef{ELM}, measuring what a random projection alone buys), and a no-trace control.\\

\noindent The archived controls show the expected task requirements (Figure~\ref{fig:7m}). The shallow network cannot represent XOR and sits below chance ($0.31$, $95\%$ confidence interval $[0.28, 0.35]$ over $20$ seeds), confirming the task demands depth. The no-trace control fails ($0.54$, $[0.47, 0.61]$), confirming the eligibility is still necessary. The random-feature \abbrev{ELM} does not solve the task ($0.50$, $[0.40, 0.61]$).\\

\noindent The decisive archived comparison is between the two deep, fully-trained rules. The pure global scalar stays at chance ($0.50$, $[0.49,0.52]$), while direct feedback alignment reaches $0.73$ ($[0.60,0.86]$) and eleven of twenty seeds solve exactly. This shows that the linear representation can participate in a fully local synthetic deep rule. It does not establish the same result for the nonlinear physical primary.\\

\noindent As first run the result was a possibility rather than a reliable method. It did not reach the criterion on average ($0.73<0.75$), performance being sharply bimodal, the solving seeds at $1.00$ and the rest near chance. Two natural explanations are ruled out. It is not a feedback-alignment failure (the forward and feedback matrices stay near orthogonal for solving and failing seeds alike), nor a failure of hidden-layer learning (the hidden weights move just as far in failures as in successes). The failing seeds instead \emph{collapse} onto a constant policy, locking onto a noise-favoured action before the annealed exploration could discover that the other action is needed for half the cues.\\

\noindent This is an exploration failure, the same one met in the action-space scaling above. The bottleneck is neither the device trace nor the per-layer credit, both of which work, but the undirected exploration of the closed loop, and raising the exploration floor barely helps. Making that exploration robust is an open problem under the all-local constraint. The constraint permits each synapse only its own eligibility trace and a single broadcast scalar, yet every effective directed-exploration method needs non-local information, a visitation count, a learned predictor, an ensemble disagreement, or a policy-entropy gradient, each re-importing the per-state memory or weight transport the crossbar-native rule was built to avoid. The collapse is moreover a self-reinforcing attractor: the eligibility-gated update strengthens the prematurely-chosen action while no local signal reports the vanishing policy entropy a global critic would catch. A \emph{direct} attack on the exploration problem is therefore blocked. The collapse it produces, however, admits a weaker local remedy.\\

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/deep_all_local_crossbar.png}}
    \caption[Crossbar realisation of the deep all-local agent.]{Crossbar realisation of the deep all-local agent, extending the single-layer mapping of Figure~\ref{fig:7f}. (a) Two stacked memristive arrays ($W_1$, $W_2$) flank a hidden leaky integrate-and-fire layer, with the device eligibility at every crosspoint and the reward $(R-b)$ broadcast on one wire. A \emph{fixed random} feedback array (purple) projects the output signal back, $L_h = B L_a$, giving per-neuron credit with no weight transport, and a \emph{local homeostatic} regulator (orange) holds each hidden neuron's firing rate near a shared target. (b) One hidden crosspoint, as the single-layer cell of Figure~\ref{fig:7f} but with the local update summing the feedback-aligned credit (purple) and the homeostatic term (orange) of Equation~\ref{eq:7.14}.}
    \label{fig:7n}
\end{figure}

\noindent That remedy prevents the collapse rather than directing the exploration. The self-reinforcing slide onto a constant action has a purely local signature: a hidden unit that locks the network into one action does so by firing far above its peers, an imbalance each neuron can sense in its own activity with no global information. This is exactly the quantity the homeostatic plasticity of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} regulates. A local homeostatic term is therefore added to the hidden layer, so each neuron keeps a slow estimate of its own firing rate and nudges its incoming weights to hold that rate near a shared target. This opposes the winner-take-all collapse while leaving the credit-assignment signal untouched (Figure~\ref{fig:7n}).\\

\noindent The mechanism is principled, not a tuned correction. The correct exclusive-or policy has balanced marginal activity across the two actions, so regulating hidden activity toward balance resists the collapse without resisting the solution. It is also local, a per-row activity integrator rather than a global computation. The hidden-layer update of Figure~\ref{fig:7n}(b) is then the sum of two local terms,
\begin{align}
    \Delta w_{ij} = \underbrace{\eta\,(R-b)\,[B\mathbf{L}]_j\, e_{ij}(t_R)}_{\text{per-neuron credit (\abbrev{DFA})}} \;+\; \underbrace{\eta_h\,(\rho^{\ast} - \bar{\rho}_j)\, w_{ij}}_{\text{local homeostasis}}, \label{eq:7.14}
\end{align}
where $[B\mathbf{L}]_j$ is the fixed-random feedback projection of the output learning signal $\mathbf{L}$ onto hidden neuron $j$ (the per-neuron credit the global scalar lacks), $\bar{\rho}_j$ that neuron's slow running estimate of its own firing rate, $\rho^{\ast}$ the shared target rate, and $\eta_h$ the homeostatic rate. Every quantity is local, so the update needs neither a transposed weight matrix nor a per-synapse gradient.\\

\noindent The set-point term is motivated by the linearised fill--emission balance of Chapter~\ref{ch:model}. The idealised occupancy has steady state $v^{\ast}=\rho V_{max}$, $\rho=f/(f+g)$. This latent fraction is not identified with the fitted current offset. Linearising about it,
\begin{align}
\frac{dv}{dt} = -(f+g)\,(v - v^{\ast}), \label{eq:7homeo}
\end{align}
the occupancy relaxes toward a set point with negative feedback. Equation~\ref{eq:7.14} is a synaptic-scaling implementation inspired by that sign and locality; its target, gain and multiplicative weight dependence are modelling choices. The result therefore demonstrates reuse of one device-inspired dynamical motif, not that a measured cell autonomously supplies the complete homeostatic rule.\\

\noindent With this stabiliser the archived linear-model agent reaches $1.00$ over 20 seeds, against $0.73$ without it (Figure~\ref{fig:7m}). The internal diagnosis supports a stability interpretation within this synthetic configuration. A physical-primary rerun and circuit realisation would be needed before promoting it to a device result.\\

\noindent That the credit term alone collapses and homeostasis rescues it is consistent with the principle that associative plasticity needs a stabilising counterpart \cite{abbott2000taming,zenke2013homeostasis,zenke2015orchestrated}. The defensible unifying message is that the same fitted dynamical family motivates both eligibility and homeostatic constructions. Autonomous co-realisation in one device has not been demonstrated.\\

Physical headroom, matched exponential and linear sensitivity all reach 1.000 in the final 300 trials. Their AULCs are 0.996, 0.997 and 0.997; physical minus exponential is -0.001 [-0.002, 0.000] and is unresolved. No trace remains near chance (0.497). The earlier claim that the exponential was limited because it over-credited the near-reward distractor is withdrawn.\\

\subsection[Grounding in Dopaminergic Learning]{Grounding in Dopaminergic Learning}
\label{sec:7p2p4}

\noindent The Frank probabilistic-selection task establishes the expected choose-A/avoid-B direction in a controlled synthetic setting \cite{frank2004carrot}. Biological grounding is then treated as a distinct evidence layer, using interventions to test dopamine's causal role in policy acquisition and logged replay to test whether intact recorded dopamine can enter the proposed action-contingent update. This separation keeps the synthetic learner, causal biology and waveform replay mutually interpretable.

\noindent The strongest causal link comes from the public Coddington, Lindo and Dudman closed-loop experiment \cite{coddington2023mesolimbic}. The frozen reanalysis retains the source control ($n=9$), stimulation-after-low-preparatory-licking ($n=6$) and stimulation-after-high-preparatory-licking ($n=5$) groups and follows preparatory licking above baseline through eight 100-trial blocks. The two action-contingent groups separate by 0.727\,Hz in the predicted direction; every blockwise contrast is positive and the source-style contingency-by-block analysis gives $F_{1,72}=12.03$ ($p=0.00089$). Stimulated trials increase reward-aligned dopamine by 1.15 and 1.24 standard units in the respective groups. The mouse-only bootstrap assigns 96.6\% probability to a positive contrast [-0.061, 1.519]\,Hz. This is positive causal evidence for the thesis's biological third-factor premise: dopamine changes subsequent learning according to the preceding behavioural contingency.

\noindent Jeong \emph{et al.} provide an independent causal-inhibition check \cite{jeong2022mesolimbic}. A 428-asset DANDI 000351 manifest pins the mutable draft before the authors' public Figure~6 cohort and lick definition are reproduced across 13 mice and 82 conditioning sessions. Controls acquire 2.04 more anticipatory licks than inhibited animals [mouse-bootstrap -2.27, 6.17] and finish 2.41 licks higher [-1.60, 5.97]. The intervals include zero, so this is a directionally consistent secondary replication, not a second confirmatory test. Its value is cross-dataset coherence: dopamine enhancement and inhibition move learning in complementary directions.

\noindent The replacement uses the published instrumental-learning release DANDI 001340 (version 0.250221.0527) \cite{qu2025nucleus}. Triage finds 58,590 valid behavioural trials across 69 NWB files. Twenty-one behaviour-only sessions and truncated BSD016/p118 and BSD016/p119 are rejected explicitly, leaving 46 substantive photometry sessions and 39,020 aligned trials. The predeclared reward--stochastic-omission rule selects 38 sessions, all five mice and 33,330 trials at each of the 30, 60 and 120\,s detrending windows; this sensitivity set is not substituted for the all-session primary analysis.

\noindent Each choice is predicted before the current outcome or waveform is read. The recorded action is then teacher-forced at centre-out, and its intact centre-out--to--outcome+1\,s dLight segment supplies $\int M(t)e(t)\,dt$. Weights reset by session; leave-one-mouse-out parameters are fitted on the other four mice; hidden rewarded-port state never enters a cache or learner interface. The physical headroom gate at $V=0.9$\,V, $\tau_{leak}=10$\,s, $k=3$ and $\beta_{leak}=1$ is primary, with a linear-Erlang sensitivity and independent decay-matched controls.

\noindent At the primary 60\,s window, held-out log loss is 0.509644 for the previous-choice baseline, 0.509559 for plain dLight, 0.509518 for the physical device, 0.509563 for its matched exponential, 0.509519 for the linear device and 0.509570 for its matched exponential. Shuffled physical dLight gives 0.509644, shifted physical dLight 0.509680 and binary-outcome Q-learning 0.435047. Physical minus shuffled is $-1.30\times10^{-4}$ [$-2.40\times10^{-4}$, $-2.91\times10^{-5}$], providing a small but reproducible advantage over the label-free randomisation control. Physical-minus-plain, physical-minus-matched-exponential and physical-minus-linear intervals include zero, locating the remaining uncertainty specifically in kernel identity rather than in the integrity of the replay. Reward-versus-stochastic-omission receiver-operating-characteristic area under the curve (\abbrevdef{AUC}) is 0.817 for physical and 0.818 for linear; device/matched-exponential overlap correlations are 0.872 and 0.912.

\noindent The logged replay establishes a reproducible waveform-level bridge: recorded dopamine modulates the eligibility assigned to the recorded action without exposing outcome or hidden state before prediction, and the resulting learner outperforms its shuffled-waveform control. Read together, Coddington supplies causal action-contingent policy learning, Jeong supplies the complementary inhibition direction, and DANDI 001340 supplies trial-complete continuous dopamine. The remaining question is kernel identification: passive data do not distinguish the physical, linear and matched-exponential shapes. Outcome-based Q-learning is a positive control only, and deep-XOR remains a separate synthetic sensitivity.

\subsection[A Scaling Relation for Temporal Credit Assignment]{A Scaling Relation for Temporal Credit Assignment}
\label{sec:7p3p3}

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/retention_delay_scaling_law.png}}
    \caption[Archived linear-model retention sensitivity.]{Archived linear-Erlang sensitivity of maximum learnable delay to the model retention parameter on the sequential task (20 seeds). The apparent proportionality is task- and threshold-specific and partly built in because the same parameter sets trace decay. It is not a measured fabrication law.}
    \label{fig:7l}
\end{figure}

\noindent In the archived linear representation, maximum learnable delay grows with the model retention parameter. This is an expected simulation sensitivity, because the same parameter sets how long eligibility survives. It motivates a hardware hypothesis but does not establish a fabrication-set behavioural law.\\

\noindent A dense sweep of the sequential T-maze over thirteen retention values (Figure~\ref{fig:7l}), all resolving a finite $D_{\max}$ on an extended delay grid (from $D_{\max}=7$\,s at $\tau_{leak}=1$\,s to $638$\,s at $\tau_{leak}=40$\,s), gives its form as a proportionality,
\begin{align}
    D_{\max} \approx s_D\,\tau, \label{eq:7.15}
\end{align}
where $\tau$ is the simulated eligibility lifetime. A descriptive line through the thirteen points gives $s_D\approx15$ ($R^2=0.99$), but $s_D$ is specific to the task, representation, exploration policy, budget and success threshold.\\

\noindent The local slope rises from approximately 7 to a plateau near 16 because the fixed rise time consumes a smaller fraction of long retentions. This is consistent with established three-factor theory, where trace decay bounds the credit window \cite{izhikevich2007solving,fremaux2016neuromodulated,gerstner2018eligibility}. The simulation quantifies that dependence for this harness; it is neither a novel universal law nor a measurement.\\

\noindent Read in reverse, Equation~\ref{eq:7.15} can guide a \emph{candidate} retention range for a target task, but only after fixing the model, criterion and circuit. The sweep does not show that fabricated devices can be programmed to those values, nor that a hardware learner follows the same curve. It is best treated as a co-design hypothesis whose decisive test is unavailable in this thesis.\\

\noindent A fixed-budget long-versus-short control confirms the expected internal mechanism: in the archived linear deep model, $\tau_{leak}=20$\,s bridges 1--25\,s gaps whereas $\tau_{leak}=1.5$\,s and no trace fail beyond short gaps, including under the declared stuck-off prior (Figure~\ref{fig:7dc}). This isolates retention within the simulation, not a fabricated device advantage.\\

    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/retention_as_asset_distal_cue.png}}
    \caption[Archived long-versus-short retention sensitivity.]{Archived linear-model deep distal-cue sensitivity (20 seeds). Long model retention bridges the declared delay while short retention and no trace fail, including under the stuck-off prior. This validates the simulation mechanism, not a physical retention-programming result.}
    \label{fig:7dc}
\end{figure}

\noindent A historical sensitivity at $\beta_{leak}=1$, 0.85 and 0.54 leaves the coarse retention trend unchanged (Figure~\ref{fig:7bs}). The current refit instead gives held-bias $0.470\pm0.090$ and two near-zero values 0.836 and 0.814. The historical sweep therefore supports qualitative robustness only; it does not validate a unique field-free single-rate reduction.\\

    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{Chapter7/Figs/single_rate_reduction_validation.png}}
    \caption[Historical dispersion sensitivity.]{Historical linear-model sensitivity at $\beta_{leak}=1$, 0.85 and 0.54. The current refit changes the empirical summaries, so this panel establishes qualitative model robustness only.}
    \label{fig:7bs}
\end{figure}

\noindent The decisive device test would fabricate or select cells with different directly measured drive-off retentions and test whether a hardware learner's delay range shifts. It is not performed here. The available held-bias law is $\tau_{leak}(V)\approx2.55e^{-0.665\sqrt{V}}$\,s with a zero-bias extrapolation $[1.73,3.85]$\,s, plus two preliminary 1\,mV reads at 4.31 and 9.20\,s. These observations constrain, but do not demonstrate, fabrication tunability.\\

\noindent In biology, $\tau$ is the neuromodulator-gated synaptic eligibility window, itself tunable: dopamine acts on dendritic spines within $\sim$0.3--2\,s \cite{yagishita2014timewindow}, cortical and striatal eligibility traces persist for seconds \cite{he2015eligibility, fisher2017reward, shindou2019silent}, and hippocampal traces extend to tens of seconds \cite{brzosko2015retroactive}. Equation~\ref{eq:7.15} therefore predicts that manipulating the molecular eligibility lifetime, for instance by a Yagishita-style optogenetic shift of the dopamine timing window, should rescale the maximum bridgeable cue--reward interval proportionally within the trace-dominated regime. This arm is a prediction the model makes, not a result obtained here, and the correspondence remains dynamical (a matched relaxation timescale) rather than chemical, as flagged in Section~\ref{sec:7p1p1}.\\

\noindent The electrode substitution of Section~\ref{sec:5p-ito} is a complementary experiment that should not be mistaken for this $R_{leak}C$ test. Gold and \abbrev{ITO} stacks share the same intrinsic trap discharge $\tau_{leak}$. The electrode changes only its visibility, gold superimposing the slow proton branch that masks it and \abbrev{ITO} stripping that branch away to expose the bare relaxation (the few-second discharge measured in Section~\ref{sec:5p-ito}). That exposure is what makes $\tau_{leak}$ measurable, grounding the eligibility window in a measured constant rather than a hand-set one. It does not itself shift the retention, however, and is a measurement enabler, not the tunability knob. The decisive test of Equation~\ref{eq:7.15} remains a series of devices engineered to differing $\tau_{leak}$ through trap energetics.\\

\noindent The slow space-charge branch ($\tau_d$, tens to hundreds of seconds) is not a longer-delay alternative. It is the ionic screening \abbrev{ITO} removes, monotonic rather than coincidence-peaked, so driving credit from it would lose temporal selectivity by crediting in-window distractors and would leave the biological window. The eligibility is the electronic trap discharge alone.\\

\noindent The claim is bounded by the Chapter~5 audit: workbook/run is the experimental unit, true zero-field retention is not directly measured, and electrode substitution shows visibility rather than tunability. Electrode-engineered control of eligibility time remains a hypothesis.\\

\noindent The idea that a device's own relaxation can serve as an eligibility trace is not new, and the contribution is best stated against its precedents. The earliest is PCM-trace, which reduces the resistivity-drift power law of a two-terminal phase-change cell to the eligibility-trace decay equation \cite{demiraug2021pcm}. The closest concurrent work is Wang \emph{et al.}, who use the ferroelectric polarisation relaxation of a three-terminal $\alpha$-In$_2$Se$_3$ synaptic transistor as an eligibility trace for on-device spiking reinforcement learning \cite{wang2026ferroelectric}. An analogue-memristor actor--critic network performs reward-based learning on the same two-terminal oxide device class as this chapter, but writes its weights with explicit voltage pulses and leaves eligibility traces to future work \cite{portner2025actorcritic}. A two-terminal second-order oxide memristor drives continual reinforcement learning from an intrinsic oxygen gradient, framed as a physically modulated learning rate rather than a relaxation eligibility trace \cite{ming2026oxygen}.\\

\noindent The construction is distinct in using a fitted silicon-oxide charge-trap transient whose rise and discharge form an interval-selective kernel. The strongest current evidence is the learning-rate-robust selectivity direction and the explicit state-locality trade-off against an equal-state learned basis. The retention--delay curve is a model sensitivity, biological overlap is timescale-level only, and electrode choice improves observability rather than demonstrating retention control.

\section[Summary]{Summary}

\noindent This chapter established the silicon-oxide current transient as an eligibility-state primitive under the proposed circuit abstraction. Its equation family reduces to the canonical trace in the single-state small-signal limit, while the exact linear theory and nonlinear phase map predict a preferred credit interval and define where that approximation holds. Held-out Au prediction identifies a compressed rise and an empirically supported family of KWW and linear state-space descriptions rather than a unique stage count. Across trace-level, spiking-network and closed-bandit experiments, the state assigns distal-reward credit; across the declared learning-rate sweep, its rise--decay shape consistently selects the preferred interval while a decay-matched exponential favours the near-reward event. The fair sequential benchmark places this specialisation in context: tuned generic filters can learn faster at the frozen operating point, whereas the physical proposal supplies its temporal prior without an externally stored eligibility word.\\

\noindent The Frank task and archived deep-XOR, distractor and array-scale experiments show how the learning rule composes with behavioural choice, spatial credit and homeostasis in controlled synthetic systems. Biological grounding is now triangulated: Coddington provides positive causal action-contingent dopamine learning, Jeong independently recovers the causal-inhibition direction, and DANDI 001340 preserves the complete action--outcome--waveform record while yielding a positive physical-versus-shuffled held-out likelihood difference. The next question is which eligibility shape biology uses; resolving it is separate from the demonstrated third-factor learning link. Together with the frozen pulse-off predictions and task-specific retention--delay design relation, these results provide a measured, mathematically explicit and experimentally falsifiable route from a material transient to all-local reinforcement learning.
