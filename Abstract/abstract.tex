\begin{abstract}

\noindent This thesis develops biorealistic learning models grounded in memristive silicon-oxide devices. Biorealism denotes local learning structure constrained by measured dynamics, not chemical equivalence to a synapse. The slow subthreshold current transient becomes a computational resource for spiking neural networks (SNNs). Measurements support a SPICE-compatible compact model and hardware-aware surrogate-gradient training with sampled Poole--Frenkel conductance statistics. Whether the transient helps depends on architecture: suppressing it through homeostasis aids fully connected networks but harms temporal ones, whereas using device-constrained retention as neuronal leak produces working-memory sensitivities on recognised neuromorphic benchmarks.\\

\noindent The final contribution recasts the transient as a learning primitive. In a limiting case the trap-cascade dynamics reduce to the canonical eligibility-trace equation, so measured discharge times constrain a candidate reward-credit window. The model assigns distal-reward credit, closes a contextual spiking bandit, and learns a cue-conditioned sequence. Held-out Au prediction resolves a compressed rise and an empirically supported family of compact representations. Fair controls define where that material prior is useful: interval selectivity survives learning-rate and amplitude matching, favouring a non-zero lag, while a learned equal-state filter gains flexibility through external state and readout. Amplitude-controlled temporal-order tests also expose the architectural boundary of a scalar two-terminal readout.\\

\noindent With per-neuron feedback alignment, the trace supports a synthetic two-layer demonstration without weight transport. Biological grounding comes from three complementary public datasets: a Coddington closed-loop intervention gives positive causal evidence that action-contingent dopamine changes policy learning; an independent Jeong analysis recovers the direction of dopamine-inhibition effects; and leakage-safe replay of \abbrevdef{DANDI} 001340 connects intact continuous dLight to the recorded-action update and improves held-out likelihood over shuffled waveforms. The resulting \textbf{Conditional Go} identifies kernel shape as the next question while retaining the demonstrated dopamine-learning connection. Measured material dynamics therefore supply a biologically grounded, interval-selective local temporal prior whose implementation value depends on matching device, task and circuit timescales.


\end{abstract}
