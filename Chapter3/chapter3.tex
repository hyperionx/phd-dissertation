\chapter{Experimental Methods}\label{ch:fab}

\noindent Four silicon-rich \abbrev{SiOx} device families provide the experimental basis for this thesis. Their
layer stacks, fabrication batches, contact geometries, and preparation routes differ, so each family remains a
separate evidence unit. The fabrication and characterisation methods also differ between batches, and each
experiment therefore identifies both the device family and the measurement route on which its evidence rests.\\

\noindent Devices were fabricated by the UCL Electronic Materials and Devices group, which also performed the
spectroscopic ellipsometry, and collaborators at the University of Oxford performed X-ray photoelectron
spectroscopy (\abbrevdef{XPS}). The author carried
out the electrical characterisation and interpreted the spectroscopic measurements. This account of the fabrication is limited to the
process information documented for each device batch, which differs in detail between the batches described below.

\section[Device Fabrication and Sample Definition]{Device Fabrication and Sample Definition}

\noindent Layer stack, fabrication batch, contact geometry, and preparation route together define each evidence
unit. The four device families are distinguished first, and the fabrication, patterning, and film-verification
procedures they share follow. Nominal dimensions taken from the process documentation are kept separate throughout
from the thicknesses measured directly on witness specimens.

\subsection[Device Families]{Device Families}
\label{sec:matstack}

\noindent Every device has a metal-insulator-metal (\abbrevdef{MIM}) structure comprising a molybdenum bottom
electrode, a sub-stoichiometric silicon-oxide layer, and a gold or indium-tin-oxide top contact, and two of the
families additionally include a titanium adhesion layer. All devices were fabricated on pre-oxidised silicon wafers,
whose thermal silicon-dioxide layer isolates the bottom electrode from the substrate. Figure~\ref{fig:3v1} defines the four layer stacks and their preparation routes.\\

\noindent The two electroformed families use Mo/SiO$_x$/Au and Mo/SiO$_x$/Ti/Au stacks. A separate conditioned
family has the same nominal Ti/Au layer sequence as the second of those stacks, but it comes from a different
fabrication batch and was not electroformed. For all three gold-contacted families, the available fabrication
record gives a molybdenum thickness of 280--300\,nm without resolving their individual values, so stack
composition and preparation history define the separate families used throughout this thesis.\\

\noindent The conditioned indium tin oxide (\abbrevdef{ITO}) family uses a 50\,nm \abbrev{ITO}/25\,nm SiO$_x$/150\,nm Mo stack without
titanium. Since it differs from the conditioned gold-contacted family in several layers at once, it cannot serve as
a single-variable electrode control, and comparisons between the two are read as comparisons between stacks. Each
experiment therefore reports its sample count and evidence unit.

\subsection[Fabrication, Patterning and Film Verification]{Fabrication, Patterning and Film Verification}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:3v2} summarises the fabrication workflow shared across several process generations.
Radio-frequency (\abbrevdef{RF}) magnetron sputtering deposited the SiO$_x$ layers, whereas molybdenum was
deposited by sputtering or by evaporation depending on the batch, and the conditioned gold-contacted batch pairs
sputtered molybdenum with an electron-beam-evaporated gold contact. These layer sequences and thicknesses, shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:3v1}, are the ones the available process records fix.\\

\noindent In sputtering, a plasma struck in an inert gas bombards a solid target and ejects atoms that condense on
the sample. An insulating target such as SiO$_2$ accumulates surface charge under a direct-current
(\abbrevdef{DC}) field until the plasma is quenched, so an alternating-current (\abbrevdef{AC}) field at radio
frequency, conventionally 13.56\,MHz, is used instead. Composition was controlled either by admitting oxygen
alongside the argon or by co-sputtering silicon with silicon dioxide in pure argon, and the resulting films are in
either case amorphous and deliberately sub-stoichiometric
\cite{mehonic2012electrically, munde2017intrinsic}. Both routes were used on this platform, and the
fabrication record does not preserve which of them produced the oxide of each family.\\

\noindent Molybdenum provides the bottom conductor, and earlier reports on this platform give its roughness as
0.9--1.5\,nm root mean square (\abbrevdef{RMS}) and show that the electrode microstructure influences the roughness
of the SiO$_x$ deposited on it \cite{kenyon2019interplay}. The same reports give the oxide composition as
approximately SiO$_{1.9}$; neither quantity was remeasured on the four families used here, so both are carried over
from the process generation those reports characterise.\\

\noindent The thin titanium layer improves adhesion and can getter oxygen, so whether titanium enters the oxide is a
relevant question for the families that include it. Transmission electron microscopy (\abbrevdef{TEM}) with electron
energy loss spectroscopy (\abbrevdef{EELS}) found no titanium migration into the SiO$_x$ on this platform
\cite{mehonic2017intrinsic}. Gold provides a comparatively inert top contact, while \abbrev{ITO} provides a
transparent conducting contact without the titanium layer.\\

\noindent Top contacts were patterned during deposition through a shadow mask. This defined the square pads without
photolithography, resist, or etching, and the aperture set the nominal active area. Pads on the electroformed
gold-contacted devices were 200, 400, 600, and 800\,$\mu$m squares, and the conditioned gold-contacted batch used the
200 and 400\,$\mu$m sizes. The \abbrev{ITO} devices used pads from 100 to 800\,$\mu$m, a range wider at its lower end
than the mask design shown inset in Figure~\ref{fig:3v2}. Shadow masking avoids exposing the oxide to solvents and
developers but gives less precise edges than photolithography, so the pad boundary follows the mask aperture and not
a lithographically defined edge.\\

\noindent Thickness was measured on witness specimens instead of completed devices, and each documented SiO$_x$ run
carried two silicon witnesses through deposition \cite{cox2021nanoscale}. A patterned resist mask protected part of
the first witness; removing it after deposition left a step measured with a Dektak XT stylus profilometer.
Spectroscopic ellipsometry measured the second witness without contacting the film, giving an optical thickness that
depends on the layer model assumed in the fit. These two determinations, the physical step height and the
optical thickness, are independent, and their agreement provided the cross-check.

\section[Electrical Measurement and Conditioning]{Electrical Measurement and Conditioning}\label{sec:3elec}

\noindent Measured current depends on the electrical reference, acquisition path, and device preparation. The
top-contact reference fixes the stimulus polarity, the instrument determines whether current is measured directly or
reconstructed from a preamplifier voltage, and the preparation route selects the switching or transient regime.
Unless otherwise stated, electrical measurements were performed at room temperature under ambient laboratory
conditions.

\subsection[Electrical Contact and Instrumentation]{Electrical Contact and Instrumentation}

\noindent Current is the primary measured quantity and reflects device conduction and the barriers at its
interfaces. Tungsten probes contacted the devices directly because wire bonding had previously damaged or shorted
the thin stacks. Each probe tip was lowered slowly under a low test voltage, and contact was detected from the
departure from the open-circuit baseline.\\

\noindent All measurements are two-terminal, with one probe on a top pad and one on an exposed region of the
molybdenum. Every electrical stimulus was applied to the top contact relative to molybdenum, so negative voltage or
current drives the top contact negative. During source-measure acquisition, molybdenum returned to instrument
common; during pulse acquisition, it connected to the current preamplifier's virtual-ground input. This convention
matters throughout the electrical results because the transient is polarity selective.\\

\noindent Two-wire measurement assigns the measured resistance to the device and therefore includes the lead,
instrument, and probe-contact resistances. These contributions are negligible for the unipolar states, which range
from tens of kilo-ohms to megohms, and for the still higher resistances of the subthreshold regime. The asymmetric
family was measured through the same two-terminal arrangement, so its 100--300\,$\Omega$ bipolar values include
lead, probe, and contact contributions that only a four-wire Kelvin measurement would separate.\\

\noindent Two circuit arrangements draw on three instrument systems (Figure~\ref{fig:3v3}), and in every case the
top contact was driven. Switching, \abbrev{ITO}, and illumination measurements used a Keithley 4200-SCS parameter
analyser. Gold-contacted conditioning and step-voltage measurements used a Keithley 6430 sourcemeter, whose
picoammeter front end reaches the small currents the subthreshold regime demands and avoids the burden voltage a
shunt-based ammeter would develop against the applied bias. Pulse trains were generated externally and measured
through an SR570 current preamplifier and a Rigol DS4024 oscilloscope because the sourcemeter could not resolve their
timing. Section~\ref{sec:3pulse} defines the quantities extracted from these measurements.\\

\noindent Several sweep conventions recur throughout the electrical results and are defined here.
\emph{Compliance} limits current while the instrument sources voltage and limits voltage while it sources current,
and on reaching that limit the instrument holds the constrained quantity. Because the control loop responds over a
finite time, a rapid conductance change can \emph{overshoot} a current ceiling; compliance therefore bounds the
intended current, not every instantaneous value. A \emph{dual sweep} produces \emph{forward} and \emph{reverse}
branches that may traverse different resistance states.

\subsection[Device Preparation and Recovery]{Device Preparation and Recovery}\label{sec:3cond}

\noindent Electroforming and constant-current conditioning bring pristine devices into different operating regimes,
with the parameters and recovery intervals of Figure~\ref{fig:3v4}. Electroforming leaves non-volatile resistance
states, whereas constant-current conditioning leaves the subthreshold transient regime. The two recovery intervals
shown apply only to the conditioned gold-contacted devices; electroformed devices instead retain their states between
switching cycles.\\

\noindent Electroforming applies a strong field to induce partial breakdown, observed as a discrete jump into a
stable low-resistance state. The field initiates the event, while compliance limits the subsequent current,
structural damage, and filament growth. Each fresh device underwent one irreversible forming event, after which Set
and Reset transitions were cycled reversibly. Forming polarity, amplitude, and compliance differ between the two
switching families. These programmed amplitudes are ceilings rather than the voltages at which a device actually
forms, so the observed forming voltages are reported with the switching results.\\

negative current is applied at the top contact with the molybdenum as the return, and the voltage that develops
across the device is measured while the current is held. The gold-contacted family was conditioned from $-0.1$ to
$-100\,\mu$A for 100\,s, and the conditioning magnitude was selected once for each device and not varied during the
subsequent measurements.\\

\noindent The \abbrev{ITO} family was conditioned by the same route with different parameters, at
$-0.5\,\mu$A under a 10\,V compliance, after which held-voltage transients were measured, each voltage held
briefly enough to avoid breakdown. That electrically characterised sample was later examined by \abbrev{XPS}, and a
separate unstressed sample of the same stack supplied the pristine reference.\\

\noindent Conditioning leaves a persistent change in baseline current, and a day-scale recovery interval followed it
before any transient measurement began. An hour-long grounded recovery interval then separated successive runs and
cleared the seconds-scale volatile state from that fixed baseline; applying a positive potential to the top contact
shortened this interval. These two intervals together separate the persistent conditioned baseline from the shorter
volatile response.

\subsection[Voltage-Step and Pulse Acquisition]{Voltage-Step and Pulse Acquisition}\label{sec:3pulse}

\noindent Both time-resolved modes report the device current, and they differ in how that current is obtained;
Figure~\ref{fig:3v3} shows the acquisition path for each. In step-voltage acquisition a constant voltage step follows
conditioning and its recovery interval, and the picoammeter front end reads the current directly at the instrument's
own sampling interval. Pulse-train acquisition instead records the output voltage of the current preamplifier on the
oscilloscope, and the selected gain converts that voltage back to a current. This second path therefore depends on the
preamplifier gain, and no analogue filter setting is preserved in the available preamplifier record.\\

\noindent The same step-voltage path also reads the post-drive relaxation, with the large step replaced by a
near-zero sensing bias so that the read itself contributes as little drive as possible. Ten such relaxations were
obtained in one session on one \abbrev{ITO}-contacted pad, each holding $1$\,mV on the top contact and collecting
$500$ samples over about $226$\,s after the drive was withdrawn. A conduction current cannot be read without a field,
so this sensing bias is a property of the measurement and not a zero-field condition.\\

\noindent Three quantities specify the Gaussian pulse waveform: the full width at half maximum sets the pulse
duration, the amplitude gives the peak negative voltage on the top contact, and the pulse rate is the reciprocal of
the period between pulse centres. These quantities are varied one at a time, so that amplitude sweeps vary amplitude at
fixed width and period, while rate sweeps vary period at fixed width and amplitude. Every result reports the pulse
width, amplitude, and period at which it was obtained.\\

\noindent Each pulse train can leave volatile state on the permanently conditioned baseline, so every sweep begins at
the setting that leaves the least state behind. Amplitude sweeps run from the smallest magnitude upwards, and
pulse-rate sweeps run from the longest inter-spike period to the shortest, so that residual state left by one train
cannot contaminate the response to the next. Before each run the pre-stimulus baseline current is checked against the
value measured before the previous run, and the run follows the recovery interval of Section~\ref{sec:3cond}.

sample.\\

\noindent Illumination sweeps followed a dark--light--dark sequence on the parameter analyser. Because the source
wavelength and power were uncalibrated and the device also evolved under successive voltage sweeps, the experiment

\subsection[Electrical Data Reduction and Uncertainty]{Electrical Data Reduction and Uncertainty}
\label{sec:3analysis}

\noindent Negative-bias transients have negative measured currents, and figures plot $|I|$ where stated so that
increasing magnitude reads as increasing conductance. Shape comparisons divide each transient by its post-drive
maximum, so that a comparison depends on decay shape and not on the absolute current scale. Baseline-corrected
analyses subtract the pre-stimulus current, while the stretched-exponential relaxation fit estimates its additive
offset $I_0$ from the decay.\\

\noindent Forward and reverse branches of a dual sweep are reduced separately because they may traverse different
resistance states. Each candidate transport mechanism is tested in its predicted linear coordinates and retained only
when the relation is linear and the fitted parameters are physically admissible. Pulse-train reduction takes the
maximum $|I|$ from each pulse and measures cumulative conductance change
from the pre-train value. The mean of the final ten pulse maxima defines the operational steady state without implying
that the device reached a converged limit.\\

\noindent \abbrev{ITO} relaxations were fitted from the measured peak by least squares to
\begin{align}
    I(t) = A\exp\!\big[-(t/\tau_{leak})^{\beta_{leak}}\big] + I_0, \label{eq:3.1}
\end{align}

\noindent where $I(t)$ is the measured current magnitude at a time $t$ after bias removal, $A$ the fitted
amplitude of the decaying component, $\tau_{leak}$ its retention scale in seconds, $\beta_{leak}$ a dimensionless
exponent setting the decay shape, and $I_0$ the fitted settled-current offset.\\

\noindent The acceptance criteria test the measurement before they test the fit: the peak must lie within the first
15\% of the transient, and all five final samples must lie below 70\% of the peak. These two checks exclude
incomplete rises and transients that did not decay within the measurement interval, neither of which offers a
complete decay for the fit to describe. Accepted fits then require $R^2>0.70$ and
$0.25<\beta_{leak}<1.9$, which rejects poor descriptions of the fitted interval together with exponents outside the
range the retained population occupies.\\

\noindent A tighter screen applies where one fitted relaxation supplies a decay kernel instead of joining the
population, because a kernel must describe the complete decay and not merely the interval accepted for it. Such a
measurement must run longer than $150$\,s, and its fit with the background held fixed must reach $R^2>0.95$.\\

\noindent Field-free retention was estimated by regressing $\ln(1/\tau_{leak})$ against $\sqrt{|V|}$ and
extrapolating to zero field. Each of $10\,000$ bootstrap samples drew 53 fits with replacement, refitted the
field relation, and retained the zero-field intercept; the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of those intercepts defined
the interval. This resampling propagated trace-level variation across the 53 accepted transients without assuming a
parametric distribution. The interval therefore bounds the variation within that population of transients and does
not quantify device-to-device uncertainty.

\section[Summary]{Summary}

\noindent Stack, fabrication batch, contact geometry, and preparation route together define each of the four
\abbrev{SiOx} families. Every experiment identifies the family and the acquisition path on which its evidence rests.
Electroforming and constant-current conditioning separate persistent switching from the conditioned relaxation, so
the two regimes enter the later chapters as distinct evidence units, and no single element supplies both.\\

\noindent Time-resolved data come from three acquisition paths: a voltage step, a pulse train, and a near-zero-bias
read. Sweep-branch separation, fit-acceptance criteria, and bootstrap intervals then bound what each measurement
supports. Two-wire probing assigns lead and contact
resistance to the device, the spectroscopy is \emph{ex situ}, and the family comparisons differ in more than one layer
at a time. These methods therefore identify no unique microscopic process, and the limits they impose travel with the
measured quantities into the network and learning chapters.
