\section[Motivation]{Motivation}

Neuromorphic computing aims to bridge neuroscience and artificial intelligence by emulating the principles of biological neural systems. It poses a central research question: how can biorealistic learning be implemented on memristive networks to build energy-efficient, scalable, and adaptive computing architectures? \\

\noindent Traditional von Neumann architectures \cite{von1993first} separate memory from processing. They therefore handle inefficiently the tasks the brain performs effortlessly, such as pattern recognition, sensory integration, and decision making. The brain achieves these at remarkable energy efficiency because computation and memory are tightly coupled in its circuits. Mimicking this coupling in silicon is the guiding principle of neuromorphic engineering \cite{saighi2015plasticity}. Memristors, or memory resistors, are two-terminal non-volatile devices that emulate synaptic plasticity by adjusting their conductance according to the history of applied voltage and current. This suits learning rules such as Hebbian learning \cite{hebb2005organization} and spike-timing-dependent plasticity (\abbrevdef{STDP}) \cite{markram1997regulation}. In crossbar arrays they perform in-memory computation that cuts the power and latency of conventional data transfer. \\

\noindent This thesis examines biorealistic learning and its physical realisation on such networks. Its central argument is that the intrinsic dynamics of silicon-oxide memristors do not merely host biologically inspired algorithms but \emph{instantiate} them. The device's own slow relaxation supplies the eligibility trace and homeostatic regulation that biological learning relies on. Learning behaviour therefore emerges from the material rather than being imposed on it.

\section[Key Contributions]{Key Contributions}

\noindent The contributions build in four stages, from device to learning system. Chapter 2 lays the conceptual groundwork. It reviews neuronal physiology, neuron models, and the device physics that links biological synapses to memristive crossbars. The substantive work begins in Chapter 3.\\

\noindent The experimental core comes first. Chapter 3 fabricates and characterises silicon-rich \abbrevdef{SiOx} memristors in a metal--insulator--metal (\abbrevdef{MIM}) stack deposited by radio-frequency (\abbrevdef{RF}) sputtering. It establishes reproducible unipolar and bipolar switching, multi-level states, and a trap-assisted-tunnelling account of transport. Chapter 4 then isolates the subthreshold current transient as a distinct operating mode. The transient supports analogue potentiation and depression under a \emph{single} voltage polarity. This removes the bipolar-pulse requirement that conventional memristive synapses inherit from non-volatile switching \cite{mannion2023unipolar}.\\

\noindent Chapter 5 turns this into a model. Electrode substitution and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (\abbrev{XPS}) depth profiling trace the transient to a proton-driven electrochemical reduction at the top interface. This reduction decouples the fast charge-trapping rise from the slower space-charge decay. On that basis the chapter extends the earlier empirical Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis (\abbrevdef{SPICE}) compatible compact model \cite{mannion2023compact} into a dispersive trap-cascade form. The extended model reproduces the measured transients from a single coefficient set across 0.8--1.5\,V (median $R^2 = 0.93$). It thus gives a circuit-level handle on a regime previously treated as device noise.\\

\noindent The final two chapters put the transient's slow relaxation to work in opposite ways. Chapter 6 builds a hardware-aware training framework for memristive spiking neural networks (\abbrevdef{SNN}s), and finds that whether the transient helps depends on the network architecture. It injects the empirical Poole--Frenkel I--V nonlinearity into both passes of a surrogate-gradient backpropagation-through-time (\abbrevdef{BPTT}) learner. A homeostatic gain regulariser then suppresses the transient's variability, improving generalisation on fully connected networks but reversing to a cost once the network is temporal. The opposite strategy uses the transient: the measured trap-discharge time becomes the leak of a leaky integrate-and-fire membrane, whose retention sets a fabrication-controlled working-memory hold-time and clears recognised neuromorphic and biosignal benchmarks.\\

\noindent Chapter 7 puts that same slow relaxation to a different use. The trap-cascade dynamics of the Chapter 5 model reduce to the canonical eligibility trace of reward-modulated learning. The trap-discharge retention time therefore becomes a programmable, device-set credit-assignment window, while the trap-filling rise makes the trace interval-selective rather than recency-weighted. On this footing the device trace assigns distal-reward credit in a leaky integrate-and-fire network, closes a contextual spiking-bandit reinforcement-learning (\abbrevdef{RL}) loop, and learns a cue-conditioned policy on a sequential T-maze. It reaches ceiling alongside the established R-STDP and e-prop traces, but supplies its retention as a fabrication-set physical parameter rather than a hyperparameter.\\

\noindent Paired with a per-neuron feedback-alignment signal, the trace then carries credit through a deep, two-layer network with no weight transport. It reuses the Chapter 6 homeostatic mechanism to keep this all-local learner reliable. A single device family therefore supplies both of biology's slow local learning primitives. The rule is finally grounded in dopaminergic learning. It reproduces the human positive- versus negative-feedback asymmetry on the Frank probabilistic-selection task. As the capstone, the full stack learns under a reward decoded from a directly measured dopamine transient, the neuromodulator the three-factor rule names -- fibre-photometry recordings made in mice, reused from a public dataset rather than measured in this work -- with a lower-fidelity human-\abbrev{EEG} correlate, also from a public dataset, as its precursor. Across every task a testable retention--delay scaling relation, usable as a fabrication design rule, links the device timescale to the learnable delay.\\

\noindent These contributions trace a single thread. A regime earlier work treated as device noise is recast as a usable computational primitive (Chapter 4), characterised and modelled at circuit level (Chapter 5), and then exploited algorithmically, both through hardware-aware training (Chapter 6) and as an eligibility trace for reinforcement learning (Chapter 7). The thread closes on itself. The homeostatic regulariser introduced in Chapter 6 to stabilise inference returns in Chapter 7 as what makes deep, fully local reward learning reliable. Underlying it all is a single proposition. Learning rules need not merely be implemented in hardware but can emerge from the intrinsic dynamics of matter, the device's own trap relaxation serving as an eligibility trace rather than a stored weight. The full record of publications, presentations, and awards is given in the Impact section. Chapter 8 returns to this thread to revisit each contribution and identify the remaining gap towards on-chip learning.
