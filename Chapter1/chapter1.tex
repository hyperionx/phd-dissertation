\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}  %Title of the First Chapter

\section[Motivation and Aims]{Motivation and Aims}

\noindent Synaptic learning links activity to a consequence that may become known only after the responsible
spikes have passed. Each participating synapse therefore needs a temporary state that preserves recent activity
until feedback arrives \cite{izhikevich2007solving}, and whatever survives of that state then determines the
persistent weight change. Most spiking neural network (\abbrevdef{SNN}) models represent the temporary state as
a numerical variable with a chosen lifetime, and software advances that variable between activity and feedback
even where spikes carry the signals between neurons. A physical synapse could instead retain and release the
same information through its material dynamics.\\

\noindent Memristive devices exhibit persistent conductance and volatile state in different operating regimes.
Programmed conductance stores a crossbar weight and contributes directly to weighted sums
\cite{von1993first, saighi2015plasticity}, and timed pulses can change that weight through Hebbian learning
\cite{hebb2005organization} or spike-timing-dependent plasticity (\abbrevdef{STDP})
\cite{markram1997regulation}. Below non-volatile switching, silicon-rich, sub-stoichiometric silicon oxide
(\abbrevdef{SiOx}) also produces a current transient that relaxes after electrical stimulation, a response
previously studied mainly in oxide reliability \cite{ghibaudo1999emerging, saha2001transient}. This decay, and
not the switching event above it, provides a measured candidate for the temporary synaptic state.\\

\noindent Biorealistic learning here is defined by where information is held and how each synapse updates.
Recent activity remains local to the synapse, a delayed scalar signal converts the surviving state into a
weight change, and multilayer learning proceeds without an exact copy of downstream weights
\cite{grossberg1987competitive, lillicrap2016random}. Any workable scheme must also regulate each neuron's
firing activity, since neither the surviving state nor the delayed signal carries usable information once a
population saturates or falls silent. The measured device relaxation sets the time course of the
synapse-local state.\\

\noindent The material and surrounding circuitry supply different parts of this process
\cite{zhang2020brain, mehonic2024review}. Device measurements define the static current response and the decay
of the volatile state, while circuits must write and sense the state, distribute the delayed signal, and program
the persistent weight \cite{chicca2020recipe, payvand2019spike}. The static response and volatile relaxation come
from related but separate SiO$_x$ device families, and they enter the simulations as different quantities. These
simulations therefore test measured device dynamics within learning systems rather than demonstrate an integrated
hardware learner \cite{prezioso2015training, valentian2019fully}.\\

\noindent This thesis asks whether intrinsic SiO$_x$ dynamics can provide the local temporary state through
which a network learns from spike history. The device studies distinguish electroformed switching from the
conditioned subthreshold transient, constrain the contact-associated changes, and reduce the transient to a
compact model. Network studies then place measured static non-idealities and volatile relaxation into spiking
simulations, testing classification, delayed memory, and the distribution of temporary state across layers. The
learning studies identify the relaxation as an eligibility kernel and test how its shape assigns delayed credit
in local and multilayer reinforcement learning (\abbrevdef{RL}) without exact weight transport.

\section[Thesis Outline]{Thesis Outline}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{ch:foundations} defines the neural, device, and system concepts used throughout the
thesis. Chapter~\ref{ch:fab} describes the four device families and their shared fabrication,
characterisation, and analysis methods. Chapter~\ref{ch:transients} separates electroformed switching from the
conditioned transient and measures how pulse amplitude and pulse spacing control its response.
Chapter~\ref{ch:model} then uses contact comparison, spectroscopy, and compact modelling to represent the
transient's rise and decay.\\

\noindent Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} incorporates measured static and volatile device behaviour into spiking-network
simulations, including delayed classification and layer-wise tests of the temporary state.
Chapter~\ref{ch:learning} identifies the relaxation as an eligibility kernel, applies it to temporal and
multilayer reinforcement learning, and establishes how far the observation window and the background convention
move both the fitted dispersion and the learned credit. Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} states the supported
conclusions and the integrated experiment needed to test the complete physical learning process.
