\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section[Future Directions]{Future Directions}
\label{sec:8p1}

\noindent The thesis built one pipeline end to end: fabricated silicon-oxide memristors (Chapters~\ref{ch:fab}--\ref{ch:transients}) condensed into a compact \abbrev{SPICE} model (Chapter~\ref{ch:model}), serving first as the device-physics forward map of a hardware-aware spiking-network training framework stabilised by a homeostatic regulariser (Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}), then as the eligibility trace of a reward-modulated learning rule (Chapter~\ref{ch:learning}). This section gathers the work pointing beyond that pipeline rather than within it.\\

\noindent It has three parts. The first is a set of exploratory closed-loop probes that map the edges of the device primitive, each with its bounding caveat. The second is a predicted device-internal mechanism that folds the reward-gated write into the cell itself. The third is the on-chip learning programme the whole thesis builds toward, where the ex-situ model, the homeostatic stabiliser, and the device eligibility trace would meet on real hardware. None is load-bearing. Each records a direction the work opens.

\subsection[Exploratory Closed-Loop Extensions]{Exploratory Closed-Loop Extensions}
\label{sec:8p1p1}

\noindent Five further probes map the boundaries of the closed-loop primitive of Chapter~\ref{ch:learning}. They run under the same pre-registration as the main results but are kept separate, each with a stated caveat. Two are positive-with-caveat, one is suggestive, and two are positive only in simulation. They establish where the device trace extends cleanly and where it does not.\\

\noindent The first asks whether credit reaches over a longer trajectory. In an archived linear-model exploration, the trace holds across $L=3$--12 while an e-prop-style comparator declines and no trace stays at chance (Figure~\ref{fig:8a}). Because this probe predates the fair per-method rate protocol, it is retained as a hypothesis-generating sensitivity rather than a method ranking.\\

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Chapter8/Figs/long_horizon.png}}
    \caption[Archived longer-horizon sensitivity.]{Archived linear-model longer-horizon sensitivity (12 seeds). The comparison predates per-method update calibration and is not a fair method-ranking result.}
    \label{fig:8a}
\end{figure}

\noindent The second asks whether a signed eligibility rule can \emph{unlearn}. On a reversal task that inverts the reward contingency midway, the device trace acquires the first policy and re-acquires the inverted one to the same ceiling ($0.99$, 20 seeds), so it tracks a changing contingency rather than acting as a one-shot associator. Re-acquisition is roughly $13\times$ slower than for a memoryless abstract trace ($\approx$1570 against $\approx$120 trials), because the retention that bridges delay also preserves the now-stale association it must overwrite (Figure~\ref{fig:8b}). Retention thus buys credit-assignment reach at a measurable cost in flexibility, a trade-off intrinsic to a slow physical trace rather than a defect of the implementation.\\

\begin{figure}[htbp!]
    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Chapter8/Figs/reversal.png}}
    \caption[Reversal learning and the retention--flexibility trade-off.]{Reversal learning (20 seeds). (a) The device trace acquires, then re-acquires the inverted contingency after the reversal (dashed line) to the same ceiling ($0.99$); the no-trace control stays at chance. (b) Post-reversal recovery: the device trace re-acquires $\approx$$13\times$ more slowly than a memoryless abstract trace, the cost of the retention bridging delay.}
    \label{fig:8b}
\end{figure}

\noindent The third asks whether the measured device-to-device spread of retention times is itself usable. A population of device traces drawn from the \emph{measured} $\tau_{leak}$ distribution learns poorly in raw form ($0.55$, 20 seeds), because the distribution is short-skewed and, through a device-physics coupling, the cascade's steady-state eligibility scales with retention ($e_{ss}\propto\tau_{leak}$), so short-$\tau$ synapses produce a weaker trace and are starved under a shared learning rate. A homeostatic regulation \emph{of the same class} as the firing-rate stabiliser of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio}, but applied to eligibility amplitude, removes the imbalance: each synapse divides its eligibility by a slow running estimate of its own trace magnitude,
\begin{align}
    \hat{e}_{ij} = \frac{e_{ij}}{\bar{g}_{ij}}, \qquad
    \bar{g}_{ij} \leftarrow \bar{g}_{ij} + \frac{|e_{ij}| - \bar{g}_{ij}}{\tau_{\mathrm{homeo}}}, \label{eq:8.1}
\end{align}
with $\bar{g}_{ij}$ a per-synapse exponential moving average of $|e_{ij}|$. This is the eligibility-magnitude analogue of synaptic scaling and divisive normalisation \cite{turrigiano1999homeostatic, carandini2012normalization}, and the first-moment form of adaptive-gradient optimisers such as RMSprop \cite{tieleman2012rmsprop}; the contribution claimed is not the rule but the $e_{ss}\propto\tau_{leak}$ coupling that makes it necessary. So applied, it recovers the task fully ($1.00$, disjoint from the raw case), close to a $\tau$-oracle ($0.99$) and above the best single retention ($0.77$; Figure~\ref{fig:8c}). The result is suggestive rather than decisive: it leans on homeostasis to compensate a distribution skewed from the useful range, so it points to a direction, not a second grounded primitive.\\

    \caption[Exploratory multi-timescale sensitivity.]{Exploratory linear-model multi-timescale credit from the held-bias workbook/run retention distribution (20 seeds). Device identity is unavailable and the result is a simulation sensitivity, not a device-population experiment.}
    \label{fig:8c}
\end{figure}

\noindent The fourth probe follows directly from Chapter~7's interval selectivity. A non-monotone scalar trace aliases one interval on each side of its peak; a full stage vector can disambiguate them because its components peak at different lags. In the archived linear model a vector readout learns this synthetic interval discrimination while scalar and no-trace controls remain at chance (Figure~\ref{fig:8d}). This is why interval selectivity connects to Chapter~8: the scalar peak is the Chapter~7 result, while vector timing is an exploratory extension requiring internal stage access that the present two-terminal cell does not provide.\\

\noindent A physical observability constraint bounds this to a simulation finding: the vector readout presupposes simultaneous access to the individual stage occupancies, whereas a two-terminal cell exposes only the single last-stage current of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1}. The intermediate stages are an internal construct of the fitted cascade, so the result motivates a multi-terminal or staged-readout device rather than a capability of the present cell. A characterisation sweep adds a practical bound: the advantage is limited by readout noise rather than stage count, so the measured $k\!\approx\!3$ already sits near the useful optimum (Figure~\ref{fig:8d}b).\\

    \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Chapter8/Figs/vector_timer.png}}
    \caption[A cascade-vector timer rescues scalar aliasing in simulation.]{Archived linear-cascade vector readout (20 seeds). The model vector resolves two lags that alias in the final scalar stage. Neither the lag pair nor $k=3$ identifies microscopic stages, and the present two-terminal cell does not expose the required vector.}
    \label{fig:8d}
\end{figure}

\noindent The fifth probe asks whether one model timescale can serve working memory and eligibility under distinct drives. A common satisfying range appears in simulation (Figure~\ref{fig:8g}), with the credit role needing longer retention. The 6--10\,s settings overlap the two supplementary 1\,mV reads but are not programmed device states; the historical 3.6-s curve should be read as a synthetic setting rather than the current 2.55-s field-law extrapolation. The result is shared-timescale sufficiency in a model, not one physical signal autonomously doing both jobs.\\

    \label{fig:8g}
\end{figure}

\noindent Together these probes bound the primitive without overstating it. None changes the main claims of Chapter~\ref{ch:learning}, and each carries its caveat. In particular, the cascade-vector capability holds in simulation but awaits a device exposing the stage occupancies it reads, and the single-substrate working-memory result is robust only toward the upper measured retention band.

\subsection[A Predicted Device-Internal Capture Mechanism]{A Predicted Device-Internal Capture Mechanism}
\label{sec:8p1p2}

\noindent The tagging-and-capture reading of Section~\ref{sec:7p1p1} suggests a sharper on-hardware target: performing the \emph{capture} step within the device. The peripheral update of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8} keeps the volatile trace and the non-volatile weight in separate elements. A \emph{second-order} cell, where a volatile internal state gates a non-volatile write, would let the surviving tag commit its own consolidation. This is the mechanism behind short-to-long-term transitions across material systems \cite{kim2015secondorder, chang2011shortterm, ohno2011shortterm, wang2016diffusive}. The prediction is built from this work's own data, with the fitted transient as the volatile tag and the measured \abbrev{SiOx} multistate conductances of Chapter~\ref{ch:bio} as the captured weight. It is concrete: a non-volatile conductance $G_{ij}$ moves only when a reward pulse arrives, by an amount the surviving tag gates,
\begin{align}
    \Delta G_{ij} = \kappa\,(R-b)\, g\!\big(e_{ij}(t_R)\big)\, \Delta G_0, \qquad g(e) = \frac{|e|^{p}}{|e|^{p} + e_{1/2}^{p}}, \label{eq:8.2}
\end{align}
where $e_{ij}(t_R)$ is the device trace of Equation~\ref{eq:7.1} surviving at reward time, $\Delta G_0$ one measured conductance step, $\kappa$ a capture gain, and $g(\cdot)$ a monotonic saturating gate of the residual tag (half-activation $e_{1/2}$, exponent $p\ge1$). Equation~\ref{eq:8.2} is the device-internal counterpart of the three-factor write of Equation~\ref{eq:7.8}, one volatile state both holding the eligibility and gating its consolidation. The model reproduces the tagging-and-capture signature (Figure~\ref{fig:8e}). The committed change follows the surviving tag, and the capture window widens with $\tau_{leak}$, the device-level form of the retention--delay relation of Equation~\ref{eq:7.15}. Figure~\ref{fig:8f} gives the microscopic counterpart, the electron-level analogue of Figure~\ref{fig:7s}. The trapped-electron tag gates a non-volatile filament write through trap-assisted switching.\\

\noindent This remains a testable prediction on two counts, not a result. First, the gate $g(\cdot)$ and its parameters $(\kappa, e_{1/2}, p)$ are assumed rather than fitted. Second, the volatile tag and the non-volatile ladder were characterised in \emph{opposite} forming states, the transient before electroforming (Chapter~\ref{ch:transients}) and the ladder after the one-time, irreversible electroforming of Chapter~\ref{ch:fab}, so their coexistence in one device is unestablished.\\

\noindent The reconciliation is spatial, not a switch between regimes. The conductive filament is nanoscale \cite{buckwell2015conductance}, so the surrounding bulk oxide stays in the trap-hosting state that produces the transient. The localised filament carries the weight while the distributed bulk carries the tag. The coupling is then the filament-periphery redox that sets the switching state in Chapter~\ref{ch:fab} \cite{waser2009redox}, made dynamic: trapped charge lowers the barrier to filament growth, the trap-assisted switching of established second-order cells \cite{kim2015secondorder, wang2016diffusive}. Failing that, a compound cell pairing one \abbrev{SiOx} device in transient mode with one in switching mode realises the same write from one technology. A $\Delta G$-versus-delay measurement would decide between the two.\\

\subsection[Toward On-Chip Learning]{Toward On-Chip Learning}
\label{sec:8p1p3}

\noindent The natural larger step is in-situ (on-chip) training of memristive spiking networks. The ex-situ approach used here trains hardware-aware models offline then programs the weights, which works well but separates learning from the operating hardware. In-situ learning would let memristive \abbrev{SNN}s adapt to real-time data directly on the device, closer to biological learning. It requires progress on three fronts: device fabrication, circuit design, and algorithms.\\

\noindent At the device level, current memristors still suffer device-to-device variability, cycle-to-cycle conductance fluctuations, limited endurance, and imprecise multi-level programming. Mitigating these calls for material stacks with more linear and symmetric conductance modulation, more uniform switching, and scalable, high-yield fabrication. Devices whose switching is inherently differentiable, or whose physics can be exploited for gradient computation, would move beyond synaptic emulation to active participation in learning.\\

\noindent The circuit level is harder still. The backward pass conventionally transposes weight matrices and propagates per-synapse error signals. Realising this in hardware will need analogue architectures that compute and route those signals efficiently, perhaps through bidirectional devices or time-multiplexed peripheral circuitry, without prohibitive area or power. The non-differentiable spiking nonlinearity calls for neuron circuits with built-in surrogate-gradient functions robust to noise and thermal drift, while storing intermediate activations strains on-chip memory.\\

\noindent At the algorithmic level, in-situ operation will favour more biologically plausible approximations. The eligibility-trace rule of Chapter~\ref{ch:learning} is a first step. Its three-factor update is local, needing only a per-synapse trace, from the device's own physics rather than stored activations, and a single globally broadcast reward signal rather than a transposed weight matrix or per-synapse error. Chapter~\ref{ch:learning} already takes the next step in simulation, pairing the trace with direct feedback alignment to carry credit through a deep network without weight transport. The binding limit is exploration. The deep all-local agent learns reliably only where its challenge is stability. Making robust, directed exploration work under the all-local constraint, without re-importing the non-local state the constraint forbids, remains an open problem.\\

\noindent The rule of Chapter~\ref{ch:learning} uses reward minus a scalar baseline. An exploratory actor--critic instead learns $V(s)$ and uses $\delta=r+\gamma V(s')-V(s)$. Setting $\gamma=\exp(-\Delta t/\tau_{leak})$ is a device-motivated modelling choice, not a parameter-free consequence: the physical kernel is rise-peaked and dispersive rather than a geometric discount, and a critic still requires another weight bank and arithmetic. In the corridor simulation this construction tracks but does not outperform the simpler policy-gradient rule (Figure~\ref{fig:8h}). It is therefore a future architecture hypothesis, not a demonstrated device-native dopamine circuit.\\

    \caption[A device-motivated actor--critic sensitivity.]{Exploratory actor--critic simulation against the policy-gradient rule (20 seeds). Setting $\gamma=\exp(-\Delta t/\tau_{leak})$ is a modelling choice and the critic requires a second weight bank. \noindent The locality argument is structural rather than a speed or energy benchmark. At 16-bit precision the co-design audit counts 16 external state bits per synapse for a digital exponential and 48 for three-state linear or learned filters, versus no externally stored eligibility word if material relaxation is used in situ. This saving excludes the two weight cells, selectors, sensing, coincidence generation, reward routing, programming, refresh and memory traffic. Without a circuit those omitted terms may dominate, so no CPU--GPU or absolute hardware-efficiency conclusion follows.\\

\noindent The retention--delay scaling relation of Section~\ref{sec:7p3p3} sets a second agenda, its decisive tests on-hardware and biological rather than the in-simulation sweep. The electrode substitution of Chapter~5 makes that retention measurable. The gold contact retains the slow proton branch while the indium tin oxide contact removes it, exposing the bare trap discharge, whose rate follows a Poole--Frenkel field law (Equation~\ref{eq:5.11}) extrapolating to a field-free retention of a few seconds. Because the two electrodes share the same intrinsic discharge and differ only in whether it is masked, the decisive test is not the gold-versus-\abbrev{ITO} comparison but a series of devices engineered to differing $\tau_{leak}$ through their trap energetics.\\

\noindent A nearer gap is the device characterisation grounding this law. Retention is fitted from held-bias relaxations, so the field-free value is reached by extrapolation rather than direct measurement (Chapter~5). The trace decays at zero drive, yet a conduction current cannot be read without a sensing field. Three measurements would close this. An interrupted-bias pulsed read lets the device relax at zero bias for a chosen interval, then samples the surviving state with a single brief, weak pulse, building the decay curve point by point. A low-field temperature sweep separates the thermal and field parts of the Poole--Frenkel emission rate, so an Arrhenius fit returns the zero-field trap depth and pins the retention directly. And a contactless probe, such as Kelvin-probe force microscopy or time-resolved photoluminescence enabled by the transparent \abbrev{ITO} electrode, tracks the trapped charge with no transport current.\\

\noindent The more ambitious version drives a single \abbrev{SiOx} cell with a pre--post coincidence, waits a variable delay, then applies a reward-gated programming pulse and records the committed conductance change against delay. The eligibility-trace identification predicts the \emph{shape} and characteristic window of this delay dependence from the measured transient, while its absolute update scale and any trace-to-write coupling remain circuit- and device-dependent. The same experiment asks whether one cell can act as the \emph{second-order} synapse of Section~\ref{sec:8p1p2}, holding the volatile trace and taking the reward-gated write together rather than across two devices. This would turn the central claim from a simulation reduction into one a bench can settle.\\

\noindent Beyond these, further local rules such as Equilibrium Propagation deserve study, as does how all such rules interact with device noise and non-idealities. Device stochasticity may even prove a useful component of learning rather than only an obstacle. In-situ biorealistic learning will ultimately demand co-design across all three fronts.

\section[Closing Remarks]{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:8p2}

\noindent Chapter 1 set out the claims this thesis would defend. The chapters since supplied the mechanism, evidence, and limitations behind each. This section draws them together, from the single device to the systems built on it.\\

\noindent The foundation is the device and its model. The work characterised fabricated silicon-oxide memristors, documenting their resistive switching, multi-level states, gradual conductance changes, and conduction mechanisms. Operating them below the switching threshold exposed the central phenomenon of the thesis: a slow, tunable current transient showing combined potentiation and depression under a single voltage polarity and behaving homeostatically. This was condensed into a \abbrev{SPICE}-compatible compact model. The model captures the transient and separates its trap-cascade rise from its space-charge relaxation. It also predicts how modified stacks alter the dynamics, which enables circuit-level design before fabrication.\\

\noindent On this foundation the thesis built two system-level simulation programmes. The first is a hardware-aware training framework for memristive \abbrev{SNN}s. It injects empirical Poole--Frenkel I--V statistics into training and finds that architecture determines whether suppressing or exploiting the transient helps. Using declared retention settings as a membrane leak yields working-memory sensitivities and capability results on recognised neuromorphic and biosignal benchmarks; the near-zero measurements constrain those settings but do not make them fabricated network demonstrations.\\

\noindent The second turned from steady state to slow dynamics. The trap-cascade equation family reduces to the canonical eligibility trace in a limiting case, while its fitted rise makes the full kernel interval-selective rather than purely recency-weighted. Exact small-signal theory, nonlinear phase mapping and held-out Au prediction show that the compressed rise is real but its representation and integer depth are not uniquely identified. The trace supports distal-reward, spiking and contextual-bandit simulations. Learning-rate-fair sequential and shallow-DMS benchmarks do not show a general physical-device advantage: tuned exponential, linear and e-prop-style controls equal or exceed the nonlinear physical primary. The surviving evidence is the amplitude-robust selectivity direction and a specialised-prior trade-off against an equal-state learned temporal basis; a scalar temporal-order task makes the two-terminal readout limitation explicit.\\

\noindent Archived linear-cascade experiments explore how deep feedback alignment, homeostasis, distractors and fault scaling compose with the local rule, while the Frank task provides a synthetic behavioural check. Biological evaluation is supplied independently by complementary public evidence. Coddington gives a positive causal demonstration that action-contingent dopamine changes policy learning; Jeong recovers the direction of dopamine-inhibition effects; and DANDI 001340 preserves recorded action, outcome, timing and continuous dLight in a logged replay that improves on shuffled waveforms. The \textbf{Conditional Go} verdict focuses the remaining biological question on eligibility-kernel shape. The retention--delay curve supplies a task-specific candidate co-design hypothesis for physical testing.\\

\noindent Read together, the chapters form an end-to-end modelling pipeline: fabrication and measurement feed parameter extraction, a compact model, hardware-aware training and local learning simulations. The shared homeostatic and eligibility motifs provide conceptual integration, but their joint autonomous implementation is not measured. The principal contribution is the auditable co-design link and its boundaries, not a claim that the complete learning system already exists in hardware.\\

\noindent The work also speaks to a broader trajectory in the field. Fully integrated compute-in-memory chips have shown that memristive crossbars are now a viable substrate for general machine-learning workloads at the million-device scale~\cite{wan2022compute, ambrogio2023analog}, and adjacent platforms have extended the paradigm to probabilistic inference~\cite{le2024memristor} and physics-trained networks more broadly~\cite{wright2022deep}. Recent roadmaps argue that the next decade of progress depends on closing the gap between rapidly maturing device physics and the system-level frameworks that must absorb it~\cite{mehonic2024review}. They name hardware-aware training, biorealistic regulatory mechanisms, and validated simulation pipelines as key enablers, the three this thesis supplies. The remaining gap, translating this ex-situ pipeline into on-chip learning, is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:8p1}.\\

\noindent A companion methodological point runs through every results chapter: the most trustworthy memristive machine-learning simulations are those whose noise priors are parameterised by, rather than parallel to, the physical devices they represent. The clearest example is the Poole--Frenkel regression of Chapter 6, sampled from 53 incremental sweeps on fabricated \abbrev{SiOx} devices and reused unchanged for every simulated synapse.\\

\noindent The central claim can therefore be stated positively and precisely. Measured silicon-oxide relaxation provides a physically grounded, interval-selective temporal prior that can replace externally stored eligibility state when used in the stated circuit abstraction. Fair digital controls define its domain of specialisation, held-out model comparison defines the supported representation family, and public dopamine interventions plus logged replay ground the learning rule biologically. The thesis contribution is the resulting material--algorithm correspondence: measured dynamics, a local three-factor rule, demonstrated timing consequences, and frozen tests that specify the path to an integrated on-device implementation.
