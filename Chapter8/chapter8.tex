\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section[Closing Remarks]{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:8p1}


\noindent Biorealistic learning here is a matter of where information is held and how each synapse updates. Recent
activity stays local to the synapse, a delayed scalar signal turns the surviving state into a weight change, multilayer
credit needs no exact copy of downstream weights, and each neuron's firing rate is regulated. Measured device dynamics
hold that activity in the material and fix how long it survives, while the measured static current response carries the
weighted sum. The rest is simulated circuitry, from depositing and sensing the state to programming the persistent
weight.\\


\noindent Silicon-rich SiO$_x$ supplies persistent and volatile states, each reached by its own preparation route.
Electroforming establishes non-volatile conductance levels that survive the removal of power. Constant-current
conditioning of pristine devices instead produces a current transient which rises or falls under a single
voltage polarity and then relaxes once the drive is withdrawn. A compact model reproduces that
transient through separate filling and leakage branches, both of them empirical descriptions fitted to the measured
current.\\

\noindent Static and temporal measurements supply different quantities to the network simulations. The electroformed
Ti/Au devices define the nonlinear current response and the state-to-state variation across the resistance states of a
single device. Carrying those statistics into training costs $9.2$ and $10.4$ percentage points of accuracy in the
dense and convolutional networks, which nonetheless learn under the measured forward model, whereas the homeostatic
regulariser improves generalisation only for the dense networks tested at zero fault probability.\\

\noindent Volatile state comes instead from the \abbrev{ITO} relaxation, which carries classification across an
input-free interval. Clearing that state removes the delayed decision, and an exponential of the same implemented mean
lifetime supports $5.3$ and $6.4$~s more usable delay than the measured profile at the primary criterion. Layer-wise
interventions show further that the delayed decision depends on volatile state distributed across the network. These
results make the complete decay profile, and not a scalar retention time alone, part of the device specification.\\

\noindent Two estimates of that decay rest on different protocols. Under a held sensing bias the fitted
scale stays of order seconds, and its field-free value follows by extrapolating the square-root bias dependence
fitted in Equation~\ref{eq:5.11}. The relaxations that define the eligibility kernel were instead observed at
near-zero bias over a far longer interval, and they give a scale several times larger. Protocol and observation length
both separate the two estimates, and neither is shown to account for the whole difference between them.\\

\noindent In reward-modulated learning, the normalised \abbrev{ITO} relaxation supplies the eligibility-decay kernel.
Unit dispersion recovers the standard exponential rule, while the measured exponent gives a dispersive extension whose
weighting of newer against older events changes with their delays to reward, a predicted ordering that the role-swap
learner preserves. Identification of that kernel depends on the measurement conditions, because shortening the fitted
interval and changing the background-current treatment alter the fitted dispersion, and the truncated fit shifts
learned credit towards newer events.\\

\noindent The same kernel supports a two-layer agent without weight transport once local firing-rate homeostasis
regulates hidden-layer activity, and the network fails the task when either the eligibility or that stabiliser is
removed. This extension widens the setting in which the measured kernel operates without introducing a second
measured quantity, and the learner remains a simulation throughout.\\

\noindent The evidence supports a correspondence between measured material dynamics and learning variables, and it
bounds that correspondence too, because the two device functions come from separate families, and both the microscopic
traps and the mobile species remain unidentified.
The learning circuit remains simulated throughout, and the present measurements stop short of an autonomous physical
synapse.

\section[Future Directions]{Future Directions}
\label{sec:8p2}


\noindent Direct measurement of field-free relaxation is the first requirement, because the state keeps decaying once
the drive is removed and no conduction current can be read without a sensing field. An interrupted-bias experiment
would let the device relax at zero bias for a chosen interval before one brief, weak pulse samples the surviving state.
Repeating that sequence over a range of delays would construct the decay with far less read disturbance than either set
of measurements behind the present retention values. The state would still be inferred through current, though only
during each short read.\\

\noindent Attributing the relaxation to the contact then requires replicated, matched devices. The \abbrev{ITO}- and
Ti/Au-contacted families differ in oxide thickness, molybdenum thickness, and the presence of the titanium adhesion
layer, as well as in their top contact. A matched-stack electrode series would isolate whether the contact changes the
relaxation. Measurements across contacts and devices could then determine whether that dependence is reproducible,
whether $\tau_{leak}$ can be controlled, and whether the present uncertainty, which describes repeated measurements of
one contact, extends to device-to-device variation. Replication beyond the single devices tested is likewise required
for the pulse-train results.\\

\noindent Temperature dependence and contactless potential measurements would each test a different part of the
working interpretation of the relaxation. A low-field temperature sweep could separate the thermal from the field contribution to the relaxation
rate. An Arrhenius fit would then return an activation energy associated with relaxation, a quantity that becomes a
trap depth only once a specific emission mechanism is established. Kelvin-probe force microscopy would instead follow
the surface potential without a transport current or a sensing field. This measurement must use the
\abbrev{ITO}-contacted stack on which the kernel was identified, and the shadow-masked pads of
Section~\ref{sec:matstack} leave accessible oxide between the contacts.\\

\noindent A variable-delay, reward-gated write is the central functional experiment here. Activity would deposit a
volatile state in one device, which would relax for a controlled delay before a reward pulse programs the persistent
conductance. The resulting conductance change against delay would test whether the identified relaxation sets the
consolidation window. Repeated deposition, non-disturbing sensing, endurance, and coexistence with persistent storage
must all be measured together. Coexistence is the principal device constraint, because the transient was characterised
before electroforming, whereas the conductance ladder follows the irreversible forming event of
Section~\ref{sec:4p-switch}.\\

\noindent A second-order cell could combine these functions by allowing a volatile internal state to gate a
non-volatile update \cite{kim2015secondorder, chang2011shortterm, ohno2011shortterm, wang2017memristors}.
Equation~\ref{eq:7.10} computes the conductance update outside the cell from a sensed state, whereas in a second-order
cell the surviving state would gate the write itself,
\begin{align}
    \Delta G_{ij} = \kappa\,(R-b)\, g\!\big(e_{ij}(t_R)\big)\, \Delta G_0, \qquad
    g(e) = \frac{|e|^{p}}{|e|^{p} + e_{1/2}^{p}}, \label{eq:8.1}
\end{align}
where $\Delta G_{ij}$ is the persistent conductance change written to synapse $ij$, $R-b$ is the reward prediction error
of Equation~\ref{eq:7.3}, $e_{ij}(t_R)$ is the volatile state surviving when reward arrives, $\Delta G_0$ is one
measured conductance step of Chapter~\ref{ch:transients}, and $\kappa$ is a capture gain setting what fraction of that
step a single write attempts. The gate $g(\cdot)$ rises from zero towards one as the surviving state grows, with
$e_{1/2}$ the state at which it reaches half its maximum and the exponent $p \ge 1$ fixing how sharply it turns. A
state decayed well below $e_{1/2}$ writes almost nothing, whereas one still near its deposited value writes close to a
full step. No measurement fixes that gate or its three parameters, so Equation~\ref{eq:8.1} sets out what a
variable-delay write would measure and not a characterised device response.\\

\noindent Should one cell prove unable to support both regimes, a compound synapse could pair one \abbrev{SiOx} device
operated in transient mode with another operated in switching mode, realising the same write within a single material
system. Both arrangements lead to the same measurable quantity, the persistent conductance change as a function of the
delay between activity and reward, so the variable-delay write tests either of them.\\

\noindent An array implementation would finally expose device-to-device variation, peripheral circuitry, update energy,
and latency during in-situ learning. These costs belong in any comparison between material eligibility and a digitally
stored trace, since sensing, routing, and weight programming are required by both approaches. The present work shows
that measured SiO$_x$ relaxation can set a local learning timescale otherwise chosen as an algorithmic parameter.
A direct variable-delay write must now decide whether that volatile state can also gate a persistent update in
hardware.
